Frame finishes up close

By Akshit, founder of WallMock · 18 November 2026 · How we write our guides

Short answer: WallMock draws each frame finish the way print-on-demand frames are commonly made: brushed gold and silver, satin black and white, and wood grain on natural oak, walnut, espresso and natural wood, with a 45° mitre at every corner and the floating gap on canvas. Downloads are 4096 px, so buyers who zoom in see the grain, brushing and mitre instead of a flat outline. Your artwork is placed exactly as supplied.

Each square is a frame corner cut from a 4K (4096 px) WallMock image and enlarged 2× so the detail is easy to see; tap one to open the crop at 100%. The close-ups use one ivory wall so only the frame changes. Labs named below were checked on their public product pages on 18 November 2026; WallMock is independent of them and does not copy any one lab’s frame.

Why frame detail matters

The frame is part of what you sell. Buyers zoom into listing photos, and a frame that turns into a flat outline, or a finish that looks unlike the one that arrives, makes the whole photo less believable. WallMock draws each finish with its surface (brushing, grain or satin paint), a 45° mitre at every corner and, on canvas, the dark floating gap, at the 4096 px size you download.

Floating-frame canvas

Brushed gold

A warm gold with fine brushing that runs along each rail and a soft sheen on the side the room light comes from. The 45° mitre is drawn where the rails meet, and the floating gap reads as a dark recess around the canvas. Zoomed out, it looks like smooth metal; up close, the brushing shows. Lumaprints lists gold floating frames (it describes its gold as a metallic finish), and Printseekers lists a pure gold floating frame.

Brushed silver

A cool silver with fine brushing along each rail, a bright outer edge where the light catches it and a shaded inner edge. The mitre and the floating gap are drawn at every corner. Lumaprints describes its silver floating frame as a brushed finish, and Printseekers lists a pure silver floating frame.

Satin black

A smooth painted black with no grain or texture. The shape comes from the light: a soft highlight along the outer edge, a darker inner edge and a slight change in tone from one rail to the next, so the frame does not read as a flat black outline. Lumaprints and Printseekers both list black floating frames.

Satin white

A clean satin white with a crisp mitre and a soft shadow on the wall, so the frame still separates from a pale wall. The dark floating gap frames the canvas edge. Lumaprints and Printseekers both list white floating frames.

Natural oak

A light oak with visible grain along each rail. The grain changes direction at the mitre, as it does where two lengths of moulding meet. Lumaprints lists a natural oak floating frame with a light wood-grain finish; Printseekers lists light wood frames.

Walnut

A mid-brown walnut with flowing grain and a soft satin finish. The grain meets at the mitre and the floating gap stays dark and deep. Lumaprints lists a walnut floating frame with a dark wood-grain finish; Printseekers lists dark wood frames.

Espresso

A dark, cool brown with a subtle wood figure that keeps its depth instead of reading as black. Lumaprints lists an espresso floating frame.

Natural wood

A pale wood with fine, straight grain lines, quieter than natural oak. Lumaprints lists a natural wood floating frame.

Framed prints with a mat

Gold framed print

A gold frame around a print with a white mat: the mitre, the lit edge of the frame and the shadow the frame casts onto the mat. Lumaprints lists gold frames for framed paper prints.

Black framed print

A satin black frame with a crisp mitre and a soft shadow from the frame onto the white mat. Lumaprints lists black frames for framed paper prints.

Walnut framed print

Walnut grain runs along each rail and changes direction at the mitre; the warm white mat sits inside.

White framed print

A satin white frame where the mitre and the shadow onto the mat keep the frame visible against a white mat. Lumaprints lists white frames for framed paper prints.

Aluminium framed prints

Gold aluminium

A slim aluminium profile in gold with brushing along the rail and a mitred corner, around a print with a white mat.

Silver aluminium

A slim silver aluminium profile with a bright lit edge, brushing along the rail and a mitred corner.

Choosing a finish for your listing

Pick the finish your supplier actually ships, then compare one WallMock image with a photo of the supplier’s sample before making a whole collection. If you sell several finishes, show the same artwork in the same room in each one. Questions

Do the mockup frames match my print-on-demand supplier?

They are the same kinds of finishes labs list: labs such as Lumaprints and Printseekers list black, white, gold, silver and wood floating frames, and Lumaprints lists natural oak and walnut with wood grain (public product pages, checked 18 November 2026). Colours and profiles vary between suppliers, so pick the closest finish and compare a mockup with a photo of your supplier’s sample. On Lite or Pro you can also adjust frame width and the floating gap.

Can I zoom in on the frame in a WallMock image?

Yes. Downloads are 4096 px on the long edge, and every close-up on this page is cut from such an image. At 100% you see the mitre, the grain or brushing and the floating gap.

Which frame finishes are available?

Floating-frame canvas comes in eight finishes: black, white, natural oak, natural wood, walnut, espresso, gold and silver, plus stretched canvas with no frame. Framed prints come in wood and painted frames with or without a mat, and aluminium framed prints in black, gold or silver. Lite and Pro add custom frame colours (wood grain, metallic or painted).

Does a close-up change my artwork?

No. The frame is drawn around your file, which is placed exactly as you uploaded it: never redrawn, repainted or upscaled.

Why does frame detail matter in a listing photo?

The frame is part of what you sell. If a buyer zooms in and sees a flat outline, or a finish that does not look like the frame that arrives, the photo stops being trustworthy. A finish that looks like the real product, at the size buyers zoom to, avoids that.
