Frame colour mockups, up close

By Akshit, founder of WallMock · Updated 21 November 2026 · How we write our guides

Short answer: frame colour mockups show your art in each frame finish, so a buyer can see exactly what they are choosing. WallMock draws seven standard colours, black, white, natural oak, walnut, espresso, gold and silver, plus natural wood for floating-frame canvas: satin paint, wood grain or brushed metal, a 45° mitre at every corner and the floating gap on canvas. Downloads are 4096 px, so a buyer who zooms in sees the finish instead of a flat outline. Your artwork is placed exactly as supplied.

Each colour has its corner close-ups, cut from a 4K (4096 px) WallMock image and enlarged 2× (tap one to open the crop at 100%), then two room views: a floating-frame canvas and a framed print. The close-ups use one ivory wall so only the frame changes. Labs named below were checked on their public product pages on 18 November 2026; WallMock is independent of them and does not copy any one lab’s frame.

Why frame detail matters

The frame is part of what you sell. Buyers zoom into listing photos, and a frame that turns into a flat outline, or a finish that looks unlike the one that arrives, makes the whole photo less believable. WallMock draws each finish with its surface (brushing, grain or satin paint), a 45° mitre at every corner and, on canvas, the dark floating gap, at the 4096 px size you download.

Black frame mockups

A smooth painted black with no grain or texture. The shape comes from the light: a soft highlight along the outer edge, a darker inner edge and a slight change in tone from one rail to the next, so the frame does not read as a flat black outline. Lumaprints and Printseekers both list black floating frames. A satin black frame with a crisp mitre and a soft shadow from the frame onto the white mat. Lumaprints lists black frames for framed paper prints.

Art that suits black: Black-and-white photography, line art, ink drawings and graphic abstracts: anything with strong dark shapes for the frame to echo. On a pale wall it gives the crispest edge. Aluminium framed prints also come in black.

White frame mockups

A clean satin white with a crisp mitre and a soft shadow on the wall, so the frame still separates from a pale wall. The dark floating gap frames the canvas edge. Lumaprints and Printseekers both list white floating frames. A satin white frame where the mitre and the shadow onto the mat keep the frame visible against a white mat. Lumaprints lists white frames for framed paper prints.

Art that suits white: Coastal scenes, soft botanicals, pastel abstracts and printables shown in bright rooms. On a white wall the mitre and the wall shadow keep the frame visible, so check those at 100% before you list.

Natural oak frame mockups

A light oak with visible grain along each rail. The grain changes direction at the mitre, as it does where two lengths of moulding meet. Lumaprints lists a natural oak floating frame with a light wood-grain finish; Printseekers lists light wood frames. A pale wood with fine, straight grain lines, quieter than natural oak. Lumaprints lists a natural wood floating frame.

Art that suits natural oak: Landscapes, botanicals, warm abstracts and anything for Scandinavian or Japandi rooms; it is a calm default when you can't know the buyer's room. Natural wood, the paler grain on the right, is a floating-frame canvas finish only.

Walnut frame mockups

A mid-brown walnut with flowing grain and a soft satin finish. The grain meets at the mitre and the floating gap stays dark and deep. Lumaprints lists a walnut floating frame with a dark wood-grain finish; Printseekers lists dark wood frames. Walnut grain runs along each rail and changes direction at the mitre; the warm white mat sits inside.

Art that suits walnut: Warm landscapes, oil paintings with a vintage feel, still lifes and mid-century abstracts. Walnut warms a cream or sage wall, and it holds its own against leather and dark wood furniture.

Espresso frame mockups

A dark, cool brown with a subtle wood figure that keeps its depth instead of reading as black. Lumaprints lists an espresso floating frame.

Art that suits espresso: Dark-ground still lifes and moody paintings, where black would look too hard. Up close it still reads as wood, which helps in a room with wooden furniture.

Gold frame mockups

A warm gold with fine brushing that runs along each rail and a soft sheen on the side the room light comes from. The 45° mitre is drawn where the rails meet, and the floating gap reads as a dark recess around the canvas. Zoomed out, it looks like smooth metal; up close, the brushing shows. Lumaprints lists gold floating frames (it describes its gold as a metallic finish), and Printseekers lists a pure gold floating frame. A gold frame around a print with a white mat: the mitre, the lit edge of the frame and the shadow the frame casts onto the mat. Lumaprints lists gold frames for framed paper prints. A slim aluminium profile in gold with brushing along the rail and a mitred corner, around a print with a white mat.

Art that suits gold: Classical still lifes, florals, warm abstracts and anything shown on a dark wall: gold lifts the edge off burgundy, navy or green. The three close-ups are floating-frame canvas, a wood framed print and a slim aluminium framed print.

Silver frame mockups

A cool silver with fine brushing along each rail, a bright outer edge where the light catches it and a shaded inner edge. The mitre and the floating gap are drawn at every corner. Lumaprints describes its silver floating frame as a brushed finish, and Printseekers lists a pure silver floating frame. A slim silver aluminium profile with a bright lit edge, brushing along the rail and a mitred corner.

Art that suits silver: Black-and-white and cool-toned photography, misty landscapes and rooms with grey or blue walls. Silver is quieter than gold, so it suits art that should not look ornate.

Questions

Do the mockup frames match my print-on-demand supplier?

They are the same kinds of finishes labs list: labs such as Lumaprints and Printseekers list black, white, gold, silver and wood floating frames, and Lumaprints lists natural oak and walnut with wood grain (public product pages, checked 18 November 2026). Colours and profiles vary between suppliers, so pick the closest finish and compare a mockup with a photo of your supplier’s sample. On Lite or Pro you can also adjust frame width and the floating gap.

Can I zoom in on the frame in a WallMock image?

Yes. Downloads are 4096 px on the long edge, and every close-up on this page is cut from such an image. At 100% you see the mitre, the grain or brushing and the floating gap.

Which frame finishes are available?

Floating-frame canvas comes in eight finishes: black, white, natural oak, natural wood, walnut, espresso, gold and silver, plus stretched canvas with no frame. Framed prints come in wood and painted frames with or without a mat, and aluminium framed prints in black, gold or silver. Lite and Pro add custom frame colours (wood grain, metallic or painted).

Does a close-up change my artwork?

No. The frame is drawn around your file, which is placed exactly as you uploaded it: never redrawn, repainted or upscaled.

Why does frame detail matter in a listing photo?

The frame is part of what you sell. If a buyer zooms in and sees a flat outline, or a finish that does not look like the frame that arrives, the photo stops being trustworthy. A finish that looks like the real product, at the size buyers zoom to, avoids that.
