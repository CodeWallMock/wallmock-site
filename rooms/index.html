Scandinavian room mockups

Pale oak, white linen and plain plaster. Soft botanicals, still lifes, sailboats and quiet landscapes sit well here, framed in natural oak or white; a black frame works when the art has dark lines to echo it. Nothing on these walls competes with the art, so they make dependable first photos.

Japandi room mockups

Low platforms, dark timber, linen and a lot of empty wall. Ink paintings, misty landscapes, black-and-white photography and simple abstracts suit them, in a thin natural oak, walnut or black frame. The empty wall is the point of the style, so pick art that is calm enough to be the only thing in the room.

Minimalist room mockups

Bare plaster, a stone console or a single bench. With almost nothing else in the picture, the art, its scale and its frame are all a buyer looks at. Use these for bold abstracts, black-and-white photography and any piece you want judged on its own, and check the frame corners at 100%, because they show.

Moody, dark-wall room mockups

Burgundy, navy, charcoal, aubergine and deep green walls. Art with a light ground stands out on them: pale landscapes, cream botanicals, sailboats, and a gold or natural oak frame lifts the edge off the wall. A dark painting on a dark wall disappears, so I keep these rooms for one or two photos in a listing rather than the first one.

Coastal room mockups

Duck-egg blue, washed linen, striped cushions and sea light. Seascapes, sailboats and sky-heavy landscapes are the obvious match, and soft botanicals and warm abstracts hold up too. White or natural oak frames keep the room airy; a black frame suits a piece with strong dark shapes.

Nursery and kids’ room mockups

Soft walls, cots, rounded chests and small stools. Simple shapes, gentle colour, botanicals and calm seascapes suit them; leave dark or dramatic subjects out. A light frame in natural oak or white keeps the room soft, and the cot or chest gives buyers an easy sense of size.
