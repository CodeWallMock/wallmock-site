How to space pairs and trios in wall art mockups

Keep a set balanced with consistent gaps, one group centre and artwork proportions that stay intact.

By WallMock · Published 1 October 2026

IN THIS GUIDE

  1. Think of the set as one composition
  2. Choose the arrangement for the wall
  3. Measure the visible gap between products
  4. Use group width to understand the trade-off
  5. Keep the art shape and group movement separate
  6. Make the listing tell the truth

Think of the set as one composition

Two or three artworks create a combined shape on the wall. Find the centre of that shape before adjusting any individual piece. For a row above furniture, use the group’s total width when judging the relationship with the furniture. Centring only the middle picture can be misleading when the side pictures have different widths.

Choose the arrangement for the wall

A row often suits a wide wall; a column can suit a tall narrow space. A four-piece grid creates a more compact block. These are starting points, not universal rules. Check the complete group against the actual room and keep the selection simple. In WallMock, choose from the layouts offered for that room and product rather than forcing an unsupported arrangement.

Measure the visible gap between products

The gap is the clear space between the outer frame or canvas edges, not between the artwork pixels. Thicker frames change those outer edges. When comparing finishes, check the spaces again. Equal gaps tend to make a coordinated set easier to read; an intentional unequal arrangement needs a clear visual reason and more careful review.

Use group width to understand the trade-off

For three equal-width products in a row, total group width is three times one product’s width plus two gaps. Three 20 inch outer frames with 2 inch gaps form a 64 inch group. With 4 inch gaps, the group becomes 68 inches. These are illustrative dimensions, not a recommended hanging distance. Widening gaps consumes wall space even when the artwork size stays unchanged.

Keep the art shape and group movement separate

A set may contain images with different proportions. Do not squash them into identical rectangles just to create a neat row. Decide whether their top edges, centres or overall arrangement should align. Move the composition together when adjusting position; resize it as a group when you want to preserve its internal relationships. Then check whether any piece collides with architecture or furniture.

Make the listing tell the truth

Show the exact number of artworks included in the product. A trio mockup should not imply that three separate purchases are included in one listing. Check ordering and crop on every piece, review the group at thumbnail size, and inspect each corner at full size. In WallMock, a set uses numbered artwork slots and is placed together in selected compatible rooms; it is different from a batch of independent single-artwork images.
