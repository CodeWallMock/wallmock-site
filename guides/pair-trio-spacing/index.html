How to space pairs and trios in wall art mockups

Published by WallMock · Updated 28 October 2026

Short answer: Treat a pair or trio as one composition: keep equal gaps between the outer frame or canvas edges, centre the whole group on the furniture or wall, and count the gaps in the group width. The gallery wall spacing calculator now does these sums for you, and the set of 3 wall art guide covers layouts and listings.

This guide now lives in two places: the gallery wall spacing calculator works out gaps and positions, and WallMock's mockups for pairs, trios and sets of four show a set on one wall. The rules in brief are below.

The rules in brief

  • One composition. Find the centre of the whole group before adjusting any piece, and judge the group's total width against the furniture.
  • Equal gaps. Measure the clear space between the outer frame or canvas edges, not between the artwork pixels, and keep it the same everywhere.
  • Count the gaps. Three 20 inch frames with 2 inch gaps make a 64 inch group; with 4 inch gaps, 68 inches.
  • Keep each artwork's shape. Never squash pieces into identical rectangles to make a neat row; move and resize the group together.
  • Tell the truth in the listing. Show the exact number of artworks included.

Questions

How far apart should a pair or trio hang?

About 3–6 inches (8–15 cm) between frames is a common guideline for most sizes. Keep every gap the same.

Where do I measure the gap?

Between the outer edges of the frames or canvases, not between the artwork pixels. A thicker frame changes the outer edge, so check again when you change finishes.

How do I work out the width of a trio?

Add the outer width of each piece and the gaps between them: three 20 inch frames with 2 inch gaps make a 64 inch group.
