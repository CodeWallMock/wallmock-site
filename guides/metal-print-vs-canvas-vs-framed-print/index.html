Metal print vs canvas vs framed print: which to sell, and how to show each

Published by WallMock · Updated 8 November 2026

Short answer: Canvas has a soft woven texture and no glare, and suits paintings and warm, textured art. A framed print is the most traditional, with the option of a mat and glazing, and suits illustration, line art and fine-art photography. A metal print is the sharpest and most saturated, with a modern frameless edge, but gloss surfaces reflect light. Choose by the art and the buyer’s room, offer each as its own listing or variant, and show each product’s own edge in the photos.

  • CanvasWoven texture, no glass, little glare. Stretched or in a floating frame; depth shows from the side.
  • Framed printPaper behind glazing, optional mat. Crisp detail; glazing can reflect.
  • Metal printAluminium panel, frameless. Sharpest detail and strongest colour; gloss reflects.
  • Same file, three productsYour artwork’s ratio decides the sizes; the product decides the surface and the edge buyers see.

Most wall art shops start with one product and add others later. Each one changes how the same artwork looks, what the buyer expects to receive and what your photos must prove. Below, one artwork is shown as a floating-frame canvas, a stretched canvas, a framed print and a metal print in the same room, followed by the differences that matter when you sell them.

Front-on, the stretched canvas and the metal print look alike: both are frameless and cast a shadow. The differences buyers care about are the surface, which shows up close, and the edge, which shows from the side. That is why each product needs its own close-up photo.

Which to sell first

  • Painterly or textured art (oil, acrylic, watercolour washes): canvas, and a floating frame if you want a finished, higher-priced look. See framed canvas vs stretched canvas.
  • Line art, illustration, typography and botanical studies: a framed print, with a mat if the art has space around it.
  • Photography, night skies, saturated abstracts: metal, or a framed print on luster paper if you want glazing and a mat.
  • Bathrooms and kitchens: buyers often ask about humidity. Repeat only what your supplier states about its product; Printful, for example, describes its metal print as corrosion-resistant aluminium.

Costs differ by product, size and supplier, so compare your supplier’s base price for the same size before you set retail prices. Work out your margin per size with the Etsy fee calculator and the pricing guide.

Listing all three without confusing buyers

  1. Separate listings or separate products are clearest. Canvas, framed print and metal need different titles, photos and prices. If you do combine them, make the product a variation and give each option its own photo (see Etsy variations for wall art).
  2. Keep the room, change the product. A buyer comparing options should see only the product change, as in the four images above.
  3. Give each product its own close-up. The canvas edge, the frame corner and mat, the metal edge.
  4. Say what is included. Hanging hardware, glazing type and depth differ by product and by supplier.

Questions

Is a metal print better than canvas?

Not better, different. Metal is sharper and more saturated with a modern frameless edge, and gloss reflects light. Canvas is softer, textured and low-glare. Choose by the art: photography and graphic art often suit metal, paintings often suit canvas.

Canvas or framed print: which sells better?

It depends on the art and the buyer. Canvas suits painterly art and large sizes; framed prints suit illustration, line art and gallery walls, and a mat makes them look finished. Test both with the same artwork and compare your own sales.

Do metal prints have glare?

Gloss metal prints reflect windows and lamps; matte or lustre surfaces reduce glare. Show the surface your supplier actually sells, and add a close-up.

Can I sell the same artwork as canvas, framed print and metal?

Yes, if the file has enough pixels and its ratio matches the sizes each product offers. Give each product its own photos, because the edge and surface differ.
