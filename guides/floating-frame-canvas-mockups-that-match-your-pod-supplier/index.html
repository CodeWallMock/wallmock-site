What makes a floating frame canvas look real

A floating frame, or floater, holds a stretched canvas from behind, so nothing covers the front of the art. In a listing photo, buyers read six details. A mockup that gets them right looks like the frame you ship; one that misses them looks like a framed print or a template.

  1. The gap. A thin, even recess between the canvas edge and the inside of the frame, on all four sides. It reads dark, because you look into the frame’s inner wall and its shadow.
  2. The face width. The narrow front of the frame. Suppliers list between about 9 and 14 mm (see the table below); a chunky face reads as a heavier, different frame.
  3. The depth. From the front you don’t see depth directly. You see it in the shadow and, at an angle, in the frame side. WallMock offers canvas body depths of 0.75, 1.25 and 1.5 in.
  4. The finish colour. Black, white, wood or metal, matching the variant the buyer orders.
  5. The sheen. How the finish takes the light: even paint, visible wood grain, or a metallic highlight on gold and silver.
  6. The shadow. Soft and short, on the side away from the room’s light.

Turn a supplier’s numbers into slider settings

WallMock draws its floating frame in proportion to the artwork, so it keeps the same presence at every print size. At 100%, the face is about 2.2% and the gap about 1.65% of the artwork’s short side. We measured our walnut render: a 16 px gap and a 20 px face on an artwork 938 px tall.

For a 16 × 20 in canvas (406 mm short side), the default draws a face of about 9 mm and a gap of about 7 mm. To start near Prodigi’s 12 mm face and 5 mm gap, try Frame width around 135% and Floating gap around 75%. For Gelato’s 12 mm gap, try Floating gap around 180%. These numbers fit a 16 × 20 in canvas only: suppliers use the same millimetres at every size while WallMock scales with the art, so smaller prints need higher percentages and larger prints lower. They are starting points for the eye, not millimetre settings: compare the corner with a sample photo before you list.

Customising the floating frame in WallMock

Everything below is in the live app. Choose Canvas, then Floating frame, then a finish. The swatches show what each finish is made of: Painted, Wood or Metallic.

Frame width: thin, medium or bold

Medium is the default. On Lite or Pro, Frame width runs from 60% (thinner) to 150% (thicker), so you can get close to a slim floater or a bold one. The canvas keeps its proportions; a bolder frame or a wider gap makes it slightly smaller inside the room’s placement.

Floating gap: tight or wide

Floating gap runs from 30% to 200% of the default. Narrow it for a tight reveal like Prodigi’s, or widen it for a deep shadow gap like Gelato’s.

Custom frame colours (Lite and Pro)

Save up to five custom frame colours and reuse them in product profiles. Choose wood grain, metallic or painted, then pick a colour with the colour picker or one of 21 one-click colours (5 woods, 6 metals and 10 paints). Custom colours are close approximations, not a supplier’s finish. Some rooms keep only the frame looks we reviewed for them; custom frames work in all the others.

Sheen, as it really works

There is no separate sheen slider. The sheen comes with the finish: painted black and white look even, the woods show grain, and gold and silver have a metallic highlight. For a custom colour, the material sets it: wood keeps its grain, metallic its sheen and painted stays smooth. The highlights and the wall shadow follow the room’s light, or you can set light from the left, right or even under Advanced lighting.

Save it once as a profile

A profile saves one product’s whole look: frame colour, width, depth and finish. Profiles come with every paid plan. Free accounts use the standard frames.

Flat templates vs real-construction mockups

A typical flat template is a photo with a frame already in it, and your art is pasted into the opening. That is quick for one image, but the frame colour, the face width, the gap and the shadow are fixed in the photo. A construction-based mockup draws the frame around your canvas, so those details can change with the product you sell.

  4. Choose Canvas, then Floating frame and a finish. Pick the closest of the 8 finishes: black, white, natural oak, natural wood, walnut, espresso, gold or silver.
