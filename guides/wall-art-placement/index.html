Wall art placement in mockups: size, balance and usable wall space

By AK, founder of WallMock and an Etsy wall-art seller since 2023 · Updated 14 November 2026 · How we write our guides

Short answer: Read the wall before the furniture. If the wall has panels, wainscoting or a niche, put the art inside that area, centred; if it is plain, centre the art on the room's centrepiece (sofa, bed, sideboard), about 15–25 cm (6–10 in) above a sofa back and about two-thirds to three-quarters of the furniture's width.

The hanging heights are common design rules. The order, wall first and furniture second, is what our November 2026 review of every room in WallMock's library of mockup rooms settled, and it is how the default art spot in each room is set today.

What we found in our room review

In November 2026 we went through all 518 rooms in WallMock and moved the default art spot in about 140 of them. Most of those moves had the same cause: the wall's own design (a panel, the field above wainscoting, a niche) already marked where art belonged, and the old spot ignored it. After the change we checked 3,626 room-and-shape combinations, because a spot that suits a portrait can fail a panorama.

The rest of this guide is the set of decisions that review settled. They apply just as well to a mockup you place by hand.

Read the wall before the furniture

Panel mouldings, the upper field above wainscoting or a dado rail, niches, chimney breasts and built-ins already tell people where to hang art. When a wall has one of these, the art goes inside one such area, centred, with even breathing room on every side. Never let a frame cross a moulding. Never sit it on a rail as if it were a shelf.

This also means the centre of the photo is usually the wrong target. A wide room image may hold only one narrow hanging area, like the pier in the picture above. Find that area first, then think about size.

Decide what the room is centred on

On a plain wall, the question is what the room is built around. A bed, sofa, console, sideboard, dresser, desk, table or mantel is a centrepiece: centre the art on it. Centre on the whole group, not one piece of it, so a sofa with side tables or a bed with two nightstands is measured from end to end.

A small bench, a stool, a single chair, a few plants or bare floor is not a centrepiece. In those rooms, centre the art on the wall itself, at eye level, and size it to the wall. My own example from the review: a wing chair and ottoman in the corner of a long panelled wall. The chair is not what the room is about. The art belongs in the middle of the wall, and it should be big.

Narrow panels and piers take tall art only

A tall panel or a pier between two doors or windows suits a tall print and nothing else. Put a panorama or a row of squares there and it comes out stamp-sized, because the width is the limit. WallMock now recommends those rooms only for tall art. If you want a 3:1 piece in a mockup, pick a room with a long sideboard or sofa instead; our guide to panoramic and tall art mockups covers which rooms work for each.

Keep one centre line for every shape

Every artwork shape in a room should share one centre line. A panorama rises to the same centre as a portrait; it does not drop into the gap above the sofa just because it is shorter. If you show several shapes from one collection in the same room, this is what keeps the set looking deliberate rather than reshuffled.

Keep the artwork's own proportions while you do it. Do not stretch a file to fill a space; choose a room that fits the shape.

Hang above plants, not between them

Art goes above plants, lamps and objects, not squeezed down between them. If the only way to fit the art is to tuck it between a tall plant and a floor lamp, the room is wrong for that shape. A frame that nearly touches a lamp shade or a door frame looks accidental, however good the art is.

Size and gap on a plain wall

Above furniture on a plain wall, start at about 15–25 cm (6–10 in) above the sofa back (20–30 cm, or 8–12 in, above a headboard, so nobody hits the frame sitting up) and about two-thirds to three-quarters of the furniture group's width. In our review, a gap smaller than a fifth of the art's height above a bed or sofa looked cramped. Make the opposite mistake and push the art up under a cornice or the top edge of the photo, and it looks cramped from the other side. Our guide to how high and how wide to hang art over furniture has the full tables, and the art size over a sofa calculator does the sum for your own furniture width.

Match perspective and light on angled walls

A front-on room is the easiest to judge, because the wall and frame edges are close to parallel. An angled wall needs matching perspective, a believable frame depth and a shadow that falls the same way as the room's light. Look at the whole image, then zoom into the frame corners.

Check it small, then at 100%

Shrink the mockup to thumbnail size. Can you recognise the artwork straight away, or is a big plant winning? Then open the full-size export and check corners, edges and signatures at 100%. I check every listing image this way before it goes up, and anything with a known defect is not shown. If you also crop the photo to a square for Instagram or an Etsy thumbnail, trim the sides only, so the art keeps its size and height.

One limit, said once: a staged room is a presentation, not a measured wall. Do not read exact hanging dimensions off the furniture. In WallMock, placement starts from the room's reviewed default; if you enter a real product size with an estimated room scale, check the range it shows, and pick another room if the composition does not suit the art.

Questions

Where should art go in a mockup?

Inside the wall's own design first: a panel, the field above wainscoting, a niche or a chimney breast, centred with even space around it. On a plain wall, centre it on the room's centrepiece, such as the sofa, bed or sideboard group below it.

Should art be centred in the photo?

No. The centre of the photo is often not the centre of the wall. Centre the art on the panel, the furniture group or the wall it belongs to.

What if only a chair or a plant sits below the wall?

A single chair, stool, small bench or plant is not a centrepiece. Centre the art on the wall itself at eye level, size it to the wall, and hang it above the plant rather than squeezed beside it.

Can a mockup show exact hanging dimensions?

Only if the scene has reliable scale information. A staged room is a presentation, not a measured wall.
