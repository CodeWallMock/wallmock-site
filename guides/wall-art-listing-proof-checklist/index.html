A proof checklist for wall art listings

Published by WallMock · Updated 31 October 2026

Short answer: Before a listing goes live, verify one source artwork, the actual product and finish, every variant-to-photo match, the size information and the words used in captions. Label a room mockup as a mockup and a sample photo only when it shows the physical sample. Then review the first image at thumbnail size and check the marketplace’s current photo rules.

This is the last pass before publish: a practical check that the images and listing describe the same product. It is not a guide to ordering or inspecting a print sample; use the sample checklist for that separate job. Start with a listing-photo plan if you have not chosen the image sequence yet.

1. Write a one-line product truth sheet

Before reviewing images, write down what the buyer can select and what arrives. Include:

  • the artwork and orientation;
  • the product type: stretched canvas, floating-frame canvas, framed print or another specific product;
  • frame finish, mat or no mat, and any size choices;
  • what the provider includes, such as glazing, hanging hardware or a printed edge;
  • whether the image is a room mockup, detail photo, size graphic or an actual sample photograph.

Use the provider’s current specification for the exact product, not a similar item in the catalogue. If a detail is unknown, leave it out of the listing until you can verify it. The photo naming guide helps you keep artwork and product options organised.

2. Check the art and the image file

  1. Open the final export beside the original artwork. Check text, signatures, faces, thin lines and a strong colour.
  2. Check the shape. A 3:2 landscape should not be stretched into a portrait; crop only when the crop is intentional and visible in the listing.
  3. Look for missing edges, accidental borders, low-resolution softness, compression blocks or an unwanted background.
  4. Open the image at its intended listing size and in the actual exported file, not only in the design editor.
  5. Make sure the image is the current revision and has not been confused with a different artwork or product finish.

Shopify lists accepted product-media types and upload limits in its current guidance; Etsy has its own image recommendations. Export for the channel where the image will be used instead of assuming one file setting fits every shop (Shopify product media, Etsy listing-image requirements).

3. Audit every variant against its photos

A variant is a specific combination of product choices, such as a size and frame finish. Make a small matrix and check each combination that can actually be purchased:

Switch through the options in the seller preview and confirm the image changes when it should. Shopify defines product variants as combinations of option values, and Printify supports variant-specific designs for eligible products (Shopify variants, Printify design variants). A listing with one image for several visibly different finishes should make that limitation clear.

4. Check the promise in the words

  • Title and attributes: state the real product and materials. Do not call a framed print a canvas or describe a frame as included when it is an option.
  • Size: label the size and unit. Explain whether a number is the artwork, visible image, outer frame or canvas size.
  • Colour: describe the product honestly. A room mockup is not a calibrated colour proof; use a sample photo only for the physical sample shown.
  • Room scene: keep the art and product faithful. Do not let furniture, cropping or perspective suggest a different scale or finish.
  • Alt text: describe what each image shows, such as “Wildflower print in a white frame with a white mat.” Avoid listing search terms. Etsy recommends text alternatives for access; see its current instructions for the character limit and editing flow (Etsy alt text).
  • Policy: Etsy requires an unedited photo of the actual item for most listings; a stock mockup may be permitted when a production partner prints your original design onto a standard base product and the mockup owner has authorised its use. Check the current rule for your item and make sure the other images still represent what buyers receive (Etsy’s listing-image requirements).

5. View it the way a buyer will

Check the first image small, as it appears in a search or collection grid, then open the full listing and move through every photo. Etsy advises sellers to make the first image a clear, realistic representation and flags issues such as a dark or low-quality primary image, a collage or too few images in its listing visibility guidance (Etsy listing-image help). Those are useful checks, not a promise that a particular photo will rank.

Ask someone who did not make the listing to answer three questions from the photos alone: What product is this? Which finish or size can I select? Which images are mockups and which show an actual printed sample? If they cannot tell, make the distinction clearer before publishing.

Final publish checklist

  • Correct artwork and current export revision.
  • Product type, frame, mat and finish match the listing option.
  • Each purchasable variant shows the right image.
  • Size labels identify what is being measured.
  • Mockups, diagrams and actual sample photos are identified honestly.
  • Captions, attributes and alt text describe what is actually shown.
  • First image is clear at thumbnail scale; full listing checked on phone and desktop.
  • Current channel upload rules and applicable photo requirements checked.

If the listing passes this check but you still need to compare a physical item with a mockup, use the separate sample comparison workflow.

Questions

Do I need a real product photo if I sell print on demand?

Etsy requires an unedited photo of the actual item for most listings, but a stock mockup may be permitted for your original design printed by a production partner on a standard base product, with authorisation from the mockup owner. Check the current Etsy requirements for your listing and provider.

Should every variant have a different image?

If the buyer sees a different artwork, product, frame, mat or other visible finish, show the correct image for that option. Check the seller preview by switching through the variants.

Is a sample check part of this checklist?

This checklist verifies the listing before publication. A sample check compares an ordered physical product with the listing mockup; use the separate sample guide for that step.
