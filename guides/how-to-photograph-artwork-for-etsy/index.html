How to photograph artwork for Etsy: the unedited photo, step by step

Published by WallMock · Updated 8 November 2026

Short answer: Shoot in soft, even daylight from a window to one side, or with two equal lights at about 45° to the wall. Hang or stand the art flat, put the camera level with its centre and square to it, turn off the flash, and step back and zoom in slightly to avoid distortion. For framed art, angle the light and wear dark clothes to keep reflections out. Set white balance before you shoot, so the photo can stay unedited, then use room mockups in your other slots.

Room mockups do most of the selling on a wall art listing, but one photo has to be the real thing. Etsy’s listing image rules ask most listings for at least one unedited photo of the actual item; print-on-demand items made by a production partner can use an authorised mockup instead (as checked on 2026-11-06; see are AI mockups allowed on Etsy). This guide is about taking that photo well with a phone.

What you need

  • A phone or camera. Any recent phone is enough. Use the main lens, or the 2× lens if it has one; ultra-wide lenses bend straight edges.
  • Something to hold it still. A tripod, or a stack of books and the self-timer.
  • Soft light. A large window out of direct sun, or two identical daylight lamps.
  • A white card or sheet to bounce light into shadows, and dark clothes if the art is under glass.
  • A plain wall or easel. A real background is part of what Etsy asks for, so a clean wall or shelf is better than a seamless studio backdrop.

Set up the light

  1. Use daylight from the side. Place the art on a wall next to a window, so light crosses it rather than hitting it head-on. Overcast days and north-facing windows give the softest light. Avoid patches of direct sun.
  2. Even it out. Hold a white card or sheet on the side away from the window to lift the darker edge. With lamps, use two of the same type and brightness, one on each side at about 45° to the wall and the same distance away.
  3. One kind of light only. Turn off ceiling lights when you use window light. Mixed warm and cool light gives colour casts you can’t fix without editing.
  4. No flash. On-camera flash makes a hot spot in the middle and flattens texture.

Position the camera

  1. Centre it. The lens should point at the middle of the artwork, at the same height.
  2. Square it. Keep the phone parallel to the wall. Turn on the camera grid and line the edges of the art up with it; if the top is wider than the bottom, you are tilted.
  3. Step back and zoom slightly. Shooting from a little further away with the 2× lens keeps straight edges straight.
  4. Frame it in camera. Leave a little wall around the piece so you don’t need to crop later, and include enough of the room to show it is a real setting.
  5. Lock focus and exposure on the artwork (tap and hold on most phones), then take several shots with the timer to avoid shake.

Glare, glass and metal

Framed prints under acrylic or glass, and metal prints, act like mirrors. To keep reflections out:

  • Move the lights further to the side, so their reflections fall outside the frame.
  • Darken the room behind you and wear dark clothes; what you see reflected is whatever is opposite the art.
  • Step slightly to one side and turn back towards the art, then check the edges are still square.
  • If a reflection is unavoidable, show it honestly; it tells the buyer the piece is glazed. A clean unglazed photo of a print that ships glazed would not show the item accurately.

Canvas, frames and detail shots

The straight-on photo shows the art; one or two angled photos show the product:

  • Canvas: a three-quarter view of one corner shows the depth and how the edge is printed (mirror, image wrap or a solid colour). See canvas wrap edges.
  • Floating frames: a close view of one corner shows the gap between canvas and frame.
  • Framed prints: a corner with the mat bevel, and the back if hanging hardware is included.
  • Texture: raking light from a low side angle shows brushwork or paper texture.
  • Scale: the piece on a wall above a familiar object, or held by a person, tells buyers its size better than any measurement.

Colour without editing

An unedited photo means the colour has to be right when you press the shutter:

  • Use one light source and set white balance before shooting: most phones let you lock it, and many camera apps let you set daylight. A grey card in a test shot helps you check.
  • Compare the photo with the artwork on screen at the same time. If the whites look blue or yellow, change the light, not the photo.
  • Avoid filters and portrait modes; they change colour and blur edges.
  • Read print colours vs screen before you promise exact colour in your listing.

Etsy’s page sets out exactly what counts as editing. Check its current wording before you crop, straighten or brighten the photo you rely on as the unedited one.

Photographing art to make prints is a different job

A listing photo shows the item in a room. A reproduction capture, for making a print file, needs the art perfectly flat and evenly lit, captured at high resolution, and then edited for colour, which is exactly what the listing photo must not be. If you sell prints of an original painting, scan it or have it professionally captured, aiming for about 300 pixels per inch at the largest print size you will sell (16×20 in needs 4,800 × 6,000 pixels). Questions

How do I photograph artwork without glare?

Light the art from the sides at about 45°, not from behind the camera, and move the lights out until their reflections leave the frame. Darken the room opposite the art and wear dark clothes. Turn off the flash.

What is the best light for photographing artwork?

Soft, even daylight from a large window out of direct sun, or two identical daylight lamps at about 45° to the wall. Use one kind of light only, so colours stay true.

Can I photograph art with my phone?

Yes. Use the main or 2× lens rather than ultra-wide, keep the phone level and centred on the art, lock focus and exposure, and use a tripod or the self-timer.

Does Etsy require a real photo of wall art?

Etsy asks most listings for at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background. Print-on-demand items made by a production partner can use an authorised mockup instead, and digital downloads need a preview of the content (as checked on 2026-11-06).

How do I photograph a canvas edge?

Take a three-quarter view of one corner in side light, so the depth and the printed edge are visible. Keep the straight-on photo as well; the corner photo is extra.

Can I use mockups for the rest of the listing?

Yes, as long as they show the item accurately. Use room mockups of your exact artwork for the hero, scale and alternative rooms, and keep the real photo for the product detail.
