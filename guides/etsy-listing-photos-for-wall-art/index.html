Etsy wall art listing photos: the photo set I use for every design

By Akshit, founder of WallMock and an Etsy wall-art seller since 2023 · Updated 21 November 2026 · How we write our guides

Short answer: Every design in my shop gets the same five Etsy wall art listing photos, in the same order: a hero room with the art large and clear, a frame close-up, the art above furniture for size, the set or collection it belongs to, and one accurate photo of each option. With the flat artwork, a second room and a sizes image, that comes to about 10 photos per design. Etsy allows up to 20 photos and 2 videos, recommends at least 2000 px on each side and asks for a landscape or square first photo.

I have sold framed canvas and prints made by a print partner on Etsy since 2023. I repeat this set for every new design, so a new listing is a matter of adding the file, not planning ten new photos, and the whole shop looks like one shop. Most photos below are one painting, an umbrella pine on a sea cliff, made in WallMock from one upload.

The Etsy wall art listing photos I make for every design

Every photo should answer one question a buyer has. These five answer the questions I am asked most, in roughly the order buyers ask them.

#PhotoThe buyer's questionWhat I check
1Hero roomIs it beautiful, and does it suit my home?The art is large and recognisable at thumbnail size
2Frame close-upWhat exactly arrives?The frame colour, the floating gap or the mat can be read
3Size in a roomHow big is it?Familiar furniture under the art; the sizes written in the listing
4Set or collectionWhat goes with it?The pair or trio on one wall, or the series in one room
5One photo per optionWhich version am I buying?Each frame colour or product shown as it arrives

After those five I add the flat artwork on its own, a second room with a different wall colour and a sizes image. That makes about 10 photos per design. Etsy allows up to 20 photos and 2 videos; I only go past 10 when a photo answers something the first ten don't.

1. The hero room: art large, room calm

In search, the first photo sits among dozens of other thumbnails, so the painting has to be recognisable when it is small. I choose a close room with a plain wall and one or two quiet objects, and I keep the same hero style across a collection so buyers start to recognise the shop. Busy rugs, plants in front of the frame and loud walls go later in the listing, if at all.

Etsy asks for a landscape or square first photo and crops thumbnails differently in different places, so I leave wall on every side of the frame. The crop-safe thumbnail guide works through the numbers.

2. The frame close-up, always second

The frame is part of what I sell in my shop, Antarmada, and it is what buyers ask about most: is it really walnut, is there a gap, is the canvas behind glass? So the second photo is always close enough to read the finish. WallMock downloads are 4096 px on the long edge, which is what makes a crop like this possible. I check every corner at 100% before upload, and nothing with a known defect goes in.

The frame in the photo has to be the frame my print partner ships. Before I list a product I haven't checked, I order a sample. The frame colour mockups page shows every finish at the corner.

3. Size in a room, with furniture people know

Buyers judge size from furniture: a sofa, a bed, a sideboard. A staged sofa is context, not a ruler, so the sizes still go in the listing as numbers. On a plain wall I hang art about 15–25 cm (6–10 in) above a sofa back, at about two thirds to three quarters of the sofa's width; the sofa and bed sizing guide has the rest.

In WallMock you can enter the artwork's real size in centimetres or inches. In 332 rooms it is drawn against the room's estimated wall scale, for art with a 4–60 in long side; other rooms use reviewed placement. If you sell several sizes, one clean sizes image is clearer than trying to show every size in one room.

4. The set or the collection it belongs to

Four artwork files, each made with WallMock in the same saved bedroom. Shown as reduced thumbnails.

If the design is part of a pair or trio, I show the set on one wall and say in the photo whether the listing is for the set or one piece. If it belongs to a series, I show its siblings in the same room, so a buyer who likes one sees the rest. Using the same rooms for every artwork in a collection is also what makes the shop page look like one shop instead of a pile of templates.

5. One accurate photo per option

If the listing offers frame colours or products, each option gets its own photo, labelled with its name. A buyer who picks black should have seen black. I keep the same painting and change one thing at a time, so the photos compare like with like. A photo of one option doesn't cover the others: each still needs its own accurate image.

The other slots: flat art, a second room, sizes, a video

  • The flat artwork on its own, so the whole composition is visible without room styling.
  • A second and third room that each change one thing: a bedroom instead of a living room, a tinted or bold wall. Three near-identical living rooms add nothing.
  • Sizes and what's included, as a plain chart in inches and centimetres: the frame, the mat, the hanging hardware.
  • A short video. A slow pan across a room or along the frame adds movement in a feed of still photos. On WallMock's Pro plan you can make an 8-second video from any finished image.

I leave a slot empty rather than fill it with a near-duplicate.

Etsy photo sizes

From Etsy's help article on listing images, as summarised on our free Etsy listing photo checker (checked 28 October 2026):

  • At least 2000 px wide and tall is recommended, so buyers can zoom in.
  • The first photo should be at least 635 px wide and tall, or the listing may show up lower in search.
  • The first photo should be landscape or square, and thumbnails are cropped differently in different places.

I keep every photo at least 2,000 px on its shortest side: a narrow image of mine once showed up only 1,679 px wide on Etsy. WallMock downloads are 4096 px on the long edge on Free and paid plans. On paid plans the listing kit adds crops for a 4:3 Etsy first photo, plus 4:5, 2:3, 1:1 and 16:9, alt text for every photo and a listing checklist. Etsy's details change, so follow its current help article over any list, this one included.

The unedited photo rule

Etsy's photo rules changed in October and November 2026. Here is the short version, from Etsy's listing image requirements as our Etsy photo policy guide reads them: most listings need at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background. It doesn't have to be the first photo, and one per listing is enough. Other photos may be edited if they show the item accurately. Qualifying print-on-demand items may use an authorised stock-photo mockup from the production partner instead, a narrow allowance rather than a pass for every room mockup, and digital downloads need a preview of what the buyer gets. Proactive enforcement is planned for early 2027.

Check Etsy's current rules before you list; they were still being clarified in November. If you print and ship yourself, or order samples as I do, a plain photo of the sample can be that photo. Room mockups made from your exact file are presentation photos, not a photo of the order, so they belong in slots 1 to 5, not in place of it.

Making the same set for every design

The set only saves time if it is the same set every time. In WallMock you heart the rooms for each photo role once (the hero room, the sofa room, the second room), save the frame look, then add the whole collection: every artwork is made in every saved room in one run, and the next collection reuses the same rooms. The Etsy mockup generator page shows how that works for Etsy listings, and the bulk mockup generator page covers the image counts. Selling printables? The same set works with framed prints: see printable wall art mockups and mockups for 100 printables without Photoshop.

WallMock doesn't connect to Etsy: you download the photos and upload them in Etsy's listing editor.

Questions

How many photos should an Etsy wall art listing have?

About 10 is what I use per design: the five in this guide, then the flat artwork, a second room, a sizes image and, where you can, a photo of a real sample. Etsy allows up to 20 photos and 2 videos; add more only when each one answers a buyer question.

What should the first Etsy photo of wall art be?

A room photo where the artwork is large, centred and easy to recognise at thumbnail size, in a calm room that suits it. Etsy asks for a landscape or square first photo. I put a frame close-up second.

What size should Etsy listing photos be?

Etsy's help article recommends photos at least 2000 px wide and tall, and at least 635 px for the first photo. WallMock downloads are 4096 px on the long edge, so there is room to crop.

Do I still need an unedited photo if I use mockups?

For most listings, yes: Etsy's current rules ask for at least one unedited photo of the actual item, with a narrow allowance for qualifying print-on-demand items and previews for digital downloads. Check Etsy's current rules before you list.

Should every frame option have its own photo?

Yes. If the listing offers several frame colours or products, show each one accurately, ideally with the same painting in a similar room, so buyers compare like with like.
