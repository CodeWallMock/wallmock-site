How to make wall art mockups for Etsy, step by step

By Akshit, founder of WallMock and an Etsy wall-art seller since 2023 · Updated 14 November 2026 · How we write our guides

Short answer: Place your full-resolution file, unchanged, in rooms whose wall suits the artwork's shape, in the exact frame or canvas you sell, at a believable size. I make about 10 images per artwork, hero room first and a frame close-up second, each at least 2,000 px on its shortest side and checked at 100% before upload.

I have sold wall art on Etsy since 2023, and choosing rooms, matching frames and exporting the same designs again and again was part of every collection. That is why I built WallMock, and I use it for my own listings. These are the steps, in order. They work with any tool; WallMock's Etsy mockup generator for framed canvas, prints and posters follows the same steps.

1. Start from the print master, at its true ratio

Use your final, full-resolution file. Not a compressed preview, not a screenshot. Buyers zoom, and a soft file looks soft in every room.

Write down the exact proportion: 2:3, 3:4, 4:5, 1:1, 3:1. It decides which rooms and frames will look natural. The free print size calculator works it out from the pixel size and lists the print sizes in the same family.

Never stretch or crop the art to fit a template. Someone who orders a 2:3 print should see a 2:3 print on the wall. If you sell several ratios, build the room set for the main one and add a separate image listing the others.

If the file is in a wide-gamut or CMYK colour space, export an sRGB copy for mockups; marketplaces and most browsers display sRGB, and an unconverted file can look dull or oversaturated. If the file needs more pixels, upscale the print master before printing. I never upscale the mockup itself: WallMock places the file as it is.

2. Pick rooms that fit the shape, and keep them close

Look at the usable wall, not the whole room. A tall 1:3 print needs a tall, clear wall: a pier between doors, a niche, the space beside a window. A 3:1 panorama needs width above a sofa, bed or long sideboard. Squares and 4:5 prints are flexible but get lost on a huge wall.

Keep the framing close, so the art is large in the photo, and keep most rooms calm and neutral. Which rooms and wall colours work, and how to mix them across a shop, is in the guide to room styles and wall colours.

3. Place it where the wall says, at a believable size

Read the wall before the furniture: if it has panels, wainscoting or a niche, the art goes inside that area, centred; if not, centre it on the sofa, bed or sideboard group. The placement guide walks through it room by room.

On a plain wall above furniture, hang about 15–25 cm (6–10 in) above a sofa back or 20–30 cm (8–12 in) above a headboard, at about two thirds to three quarters of the furniture group's width. Our guide to how high and how wide to hang art over a sofa or bed has the full numbers.

Size in the room is information, so keep it honest. In WallMock you can enter a real size with a 4–60 in long side; 332 rooms offer estimated wall scale and the rest use reviewed placement. Neither is a measured physical wall, so add a labelled size image. If you sell several sizes, keep one size across the room photos and show the range in that size image, rather than changing the size from photo to photo.

4. Show the exact product you sell

A mockup is a promise. If it shows a frame you don't sell, a size you don't offer or colours that differ from the print, it can win the click and lose the review.

My own buyers taught me this. Some have questioned whether the frame matched the listing image. A narrow floating-frame gap, a different wood tone or a mat that is too wide changes the product a buyer thinks they are buying. More recent buyers have said the piece matches the website or looks better in person, which is the standard to aim for.

So framed prints get the frame and mat you actually offer, and canvas gets the finish you sell: stretched, or in a floating frame with its visible dark gap. Keep one finish across a collection (natural oak throughout, say) unless you sell several; then show each finish once, ideally in the same room so buyers compare like with like. The frame colour guide shows one artwork in seven finishes and an unframed canvas side by side. For digital downloads, a thin frame with a mat is fine as a display, as long as the listing says no print or frame is included.

Before you list a product you have not checked, order a sample. A digital frame colour is an approximation.

For a real product example, see one 3:1 canvas exported in natural oak and black. The room and placement stay the same so you can judge the finish, and a 4K corner crop shows the floating gap.

5. Run three separate checks

Composition: zoom in and compare edges, lettering and signature with your file. Some template tools add texture, glare or shadow layers that shift colour or soften fine lines. A soft shadow at the frame edge is natural; a grey veil across the whole artwork is not. WallMock places your image instead of redrawing it and keeps its proportions; the exact-art evidence describes a historical digital example set, not a physical print test.

Product: compare the frame, mat and edge with the supplier's product or your sample photo.

Size: state the dimensions and whether they describe the artwork or the outer frame. A room image cannot establish a measurement. An 8 × 10 in paper print in an 11 × 14 in frame has two sizes; label both instead of calling the whole thing “8 × 10”. The testing page explains the scope of our evidence.

6. Add one unedited photo (unless you sell print on demand)

Etsy's listing image rules (updated 22 October 2026): most listings need at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background, with no editing or AI tools on that photo. Other photos may be edited if they show the item accurately. Print-on-demand items made by a production partner may use an authorised mockup instead, and digital downloads need a preview of the content.

So mockups fill the room and detail slots, and you add one photo of the print or canvas itself unless you sell print on demand or downloads. How to photograph artwork for Etsy covers light, glare and canvas edges with a phone; for canvas, how to sell canvas prints on Etsy walks through depth, sizes and photo slots.

Source: Etsy listing image requirements, checked 2026-10-29, and again on 2026-11-06 for the print-on-demand exception. More detail in are AI mockups allowed on Etsy?

7. Set up once, then make the whole collection

Done one template at a time, this is slow: open, place, adjust, export, repeat. At about 10 images per artwork, a 20-print collection is 200 images.

Set up each room and frame once and apply the setup to every artwork. That is the core of WallMock: heart your favourite rooms, pick the product and finish once, then make the whole collection in one batch. Rooms only offer the shapes and products they were reviewed for, and an unsupported combination is marked unavailable rather than charged.

8. Order, export and check at 100%

I put the hero room photo first and the frame close-up card second; after that, add other rooms, a scale view and the size image. Etsy allows up to 20 photos; I use about 10 per artwork.

Every image I upload is at least 2,000 px on its shortest side. WallMock downloads are 4K (4,096 px on the long edge) on Free and paid plans; check both dimensions after any crop. If you cut a room to square for an Etsy thumbnail or Instagram, trim the sides only, so the art keeps its size and height. Etsy's help article “Requirements and Best Practices for Images in Your Etsy Shop” lists the recommended sizes and explains how search thumbnails are cropped, so keep the art towards the centre.

Check each image twice. At thumbnail size: can you recognise the artwork at once? Drop any image where a plant or a lamp wins. At 100%: corners, edges, signatures, small marks. Nothing with a known defect goes in my listings.

Name the files clearly (artwork, room, product) so you can reuse them on your own site, Pinterest or other marketplaces.

A checklist before publishing

  • The art is at its true proportion, not stretched or cropped.
  • The frame, mat or canvas matches what the buyer receives, ideally checked against a sample.
  • The art sits where the wall's design or the centrepiece says, at a believable size.
  • A size image gives exact dimensions, artwork or outer frame stated.
  • Colours and fine lines match the file at 100%.
  • The hero reads at thumbnail size; the frame close-up is second.
  • Every image is at least 2,000 px on its shortest side.
  • One unedited photo, unless the item is print on demand or a download.
  • The same rooms and finish run across the collection.

Questions

How many mockups should an Etsy wall art listing have?

I use about 10 per artwork. I put the hero room photo first and the frame close-up card second; after that, add other rooms, a scale view and the size image. Etsy allows up to 20 photos, and our listing photo guide has a plan for every slot.

Can I use the same mockup rooms for every listing?

Yes, and a consistent set of rooms makes a shop look coherent. Rotate the hero room between listings so search results don't look identical, and choose rooms that suit each artwork's shape and palette.

Do mockups have to show the exact size?

They should show a believable size and should not suggest a size you don't sell. Use a dedicated size image for the exact dimensions.

Do I need Photoshop to make wall art mockups?

No. Template files need Photoshop or a similar editor, but browser-based tools, including WallMock, place the artwork for you. Photoshop gives the most manual control; a dedicated tool is faster for whole collections.
