Fit a 2:3 artwork to an A3 print

A3 portrait is 297 × 420 mm. Scale a 2:3 image to 420 mm high and it will be 280 mm wide, leaving 8.5 mm on each side if you centre it on the sheet. To fill the sheet edge to edge, scale it to 297 mm wide and crop 25.5 mm of image height in total. Check whether that removes a signature or part of the composition.

With a mount, start from the visible opening the supplier specifies. Use the aspect ratio calculator to compare that opening with your artwork before you export.

Check the variant for the destination you serve

Use the supplier’s product code and dimensions for the actual fulfilment region. Prodigi’s printing FAQ gives a concrete example of a print listed as 45 × 60 cm and 18 × 24 in that comes out at a different physical size depending on the lab. Its classic frame catalogue shows dimensions and fulfilment locations by variant.

Before duplicating a product across markets, record the supplier code, finished dimensions, available frame colours and whether the print includes a mount. Check EUR billing separately from where your supplier prints and ships.

Build a consistent gallery for the collection

Keep the room, crop and frame finish consistent across the featured photos. Then add one image that shows the actual size and one that shows the frame or mount detail. Shopify recommends consistent aspect ratios for featured product images so collection tiles display at the same size.

Use the size image guide to label metric dimensions, and the frame and mount guide if the visible opening is the part that differs.

Preview your A-series print in WallMock

For an A-series print, upload the artwork at the intended proportions after choosing the crop or border. If a mount covers part of the sheet, check the visible opening for the variant you sell.

WallMock supports square 1:1, standard portrait and landscape ratios (2:3, 3:4, 4:5, 5:7 and their reverses), and long formats (1:2, 2:1, 1:3 and 3:1). Choose a room that fits the artwork shape. For other print sizes such as 11 × 14 in, prepare the file to the intended print opening and check the result in the preview. See the print ratios and sizes guide.
