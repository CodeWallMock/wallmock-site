Giclée vs poster vs fine-art paper: what to say in wall art listings

Published by WallMock · Updated 29 October 2026

Short answer: Giclée means an inkjet print made with pigment, archival-quality inks, usually on fine-art paper or canvas. It names a printing process, not a particular paper or quality grade. A poster is a print on lighter, lower-cost paper, matte or semi-gloss. Fine-art paper is heavier archival stock, such as Gelato's 250 gsm archival matte (checked 2026-10-29). In listings, use your provider's exact paper name and weight, and say giclée or archival only when your provider says it for that product.

The paper words in your title set the buyer's expectation as much as your photos do. Here is what each term means and what to write. Then show the print unframed with a poster mockup generator, or framed with a mat.

Three words, three meanings

TermWhat it meansWhat it doesn't tell the buyer
GicléeAn inkjet print made with pigment, archival-quality inks, on fine-art paper or canvasWhich paper, its weight, or whether the print is signed or limited
Poster (poster print)A print on lighter, lower-cost paper, matte or semi-gloss, made for affordable decorHow long it lasts, unless the paper is named
Fine-art paperHeavier, often uncoated, acid-free paper made for art reproductionHow it was printed; the ink matters as much as the paper

Prodigi defines giclée as "fine art printing using pigment based, archival quality inks" on advanced inkjet printers, and credits the printmaker Jack Duganne with coining the term in 1991 (Prodigi, checked 2026-10-29). It describes a process. A print can be giclée on canvas, and a poster paper can be printed with pigment inks too.

Notice that "museum-quality" means different things: Printful uses it for a 189 g/m² matte poster paper, while Gelato's heaviest archival paper is 250 gsm. These labels are marketing words, not a shared standard. The paper name and weight are what you can stand behind.

Matte, luster or semi-gloss: what buyers notice

  • Matte has no shine, so it shows no glare under lamps or behind glass. It suits paintings, illustration and soft colours.
  • Luster and semi-gloss have a soft sheen that deepens blacks and makes colours look richer. They suit photography, but can catch light when hung unframed opposite a window.
  • Uncoated archival paper often looks a softer white. Gelato says its archival papers have no optical brightening agents, the additives that make paper look extra white (checked 2026-10-29).
  • Weight (gsm) is grams per square metre. Heavier paper feels more substantial and lies flatter in a frame.

Words to use, words to avoid

Every claim in your title and description should be one your provider makes for the exact product you sell.

Write thisNot this
"Printed on 250 gsm archival matte paper""Premium museum-grade giclée" on a standard poster
"Giclée print with pigment inks", when your provider says so for that product"Giclée" for any print that looks good
"Semi-gloss photo paper, 200 gsm""High quality paper"
"Art print of an original painting""Original painting" for a print
"Frame not included" or the exact frameA framed photo with no note, on an unframed product

Etsy's image rules say all images must accurately reflect the item's true appearance, including colour, condition, scale and materials (Etsy Help, checked 2026-10-29). Your words should say the same thing as your photos.

Show the paper, not just the room

Room photos sell the art and the frame. They can't show paper weight or surface, so add proof from a sample:

  1. A room photo for the thumbnail, at a believable size.
  2. A framed close-up, if you sell it framed, so buyers see the frame and mat.
  3. A close-up of a sample print at an angle in daylight, showing the surface and any sheen.
  4. For unframed prints, a photo of the border and edge, so buyers see the margin they get.

Selling digital downloads? Don't describe a paper you don't supply; the buyer chooses the paper. Our guide to mockups for printable wall art covers how to present them, and framed prints with or without a mat covers the frame choice.

Example listing lines

  • Poster: "Art print on 200 gsm premium matte paper. Unframed; white border. Sizes 12×16 to 24×36 in."
  • Fine-art print: "Printed on 250 gsm archival matte paper, uncoated, with no optical brighteners. Unframed."
  • Giclée: "Giclée print with pigment inks on [paper name from your provider]. Signed by hand on the back." (Only if you sign it.)
  • Framed print: "Matte paper print in a black wood frame with a white mat. Print 8×10 in, frame 11×14 in."

Questions

Is giclée better than a poster?

A giclée uses pigment, archival-quality inks, usually on heavier fine-art paper, so it is made to last and to reproduce colour closely. A poster is a lower-cost print for everyday decor. Which is better depends on the price and buyer.

Is a giclée the same as a fine-art print?

Often, but not always. Giclée names the printing process; fine-art print usually names the paper. Say both only when both are true for your product.

What paper weight is good for art prints?

Heavier paper feels more substantial. Gelato's poster papers run from 170 to 250 gsm, and Printful's matte poster paper is 189 g/m² (checked 2026-10-29).

Can I call my print giclée if my provider doesn't?

No. Use the words your provider uses for that exact product. If it doesn't say giclée, describe the paper and ink it does name.

Is matte or semi-gloss better for wall art?

Matte avoids glare and suits paintings and illustration. Semi-gloss or luster gives deeper blacks and suits photography.

What does gsm mean?

Grams per square metre: the weight of the paper. A higher number means a heavier, usually thicker sheet.
