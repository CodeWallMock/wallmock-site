Set of 3 wall art: presenting pairs, trios and sets

Published by WallMock · Updated 28 October 2026

Short answer: A set of 3 wall art sells best when the mockup makes the group look like one piece: equal gaps of about 3–6 inches (8–15 cm), a shared centre line, matching frames, and a group about two thirds to three quarters as wide as the furniture below. Show the set together on one wall, then each piece alone, and say exactly what the buyer receives.

Sets sell more than one piece per order, but only if the mockup makes the group look intentional. Consistent gaps, a shared centre line and a room with enough wall are what make a set convincing, and they are built into WallMock's gallery wall mockup generator for pairs, trios and sets of four.

Why sell sets

A matching pair or trio raises the order value and solves a real problem for buyers: filling a large wall above a sofa or bed with pieces that clearly belong together. Many buyers search specifically for a 'set of 2' or 'set of 3', and sets are one of the easiest ways to stand out from single-print listings.

Sets also suit the way artists work: a series of botanicals, three views of the same coast, or one panorama split across three canvases.

Use consistent gaps

Keep the space between pieces equal. In most homes, gaps of about 3–6 inches (8–15 cm) between frames are a common guideline; wider gaps make pieces feel unrelated, narrower ones look cramped. The gap should be the same between every piece in the group.

Measure the gap between outer edges

The gap is the clear space between the outer frame or canvas edges, not between the artwork pixels. Thicker frames change those outer edges, so check the spaces again when you compare finishes.

For three equal-width pieces in a row, the group width is three times one piece plus two gaps. Three 20 inch outer frames with 2 inch gaps make a 64 inch group; with 4 inch gaps the group becomes 68 inches. These are illustrative dimensions, not a recommended hanging distance: widening the gaps uses wall space even when the art stays the same size.

Align on a shared centre line

For a row of equal pieces, align tops and bottoms. For mixed sizes, align the centres horizontally. Centre the whole group on the furniture below, not each piece separately.

For a 2×2 grid, keep the vertical gap the same as the horizontal gap so the grid reads as one square block. For mixed sizes or a bigger wall, the gallery wall layout planner draws each frame to scale.

Choose rooms with enough wall

A pair needs a wide wall; a trio needs more. Above a sofa, a bed or a long sideboard is the classic place. Our gallery wall spacing calculator works out the group width, the gaps and where each piece goes for a pair, trio, row of four or 2×2 grid.

Make the series obvious

Use matching frames and mats, and order the pieces so colours or subjects lead the eye across the group. Then show each piece on its own too, so buyers can see the details.

In the listing, say exactly what is included (for example 'set of 3 prints, 30×40 cm each, frames not included'), because sets are where buyers most often misread the quantity. A trio mockup should not suggest that three separate purchases come in one listing.

Questions

How far apart should a set of three frames be?

About 3–6 inches (8–15 cm) between frames is a common guideline for most sizes. Keep every gap the same.

How wide should a trio be above a sofa?

About two thirds to three quarters of the sofa's width for the whole group, centred on the sofa.

Should a set of 3 be one image split into three?

It can be. A split panorama reads as one artwork; three related pieces read as a series. Both sell; say clearly which one the buyer receives.
