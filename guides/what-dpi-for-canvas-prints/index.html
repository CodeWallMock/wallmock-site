What DPI does a canvas print need? Pixels by size, with the wrap

Published by WallMock · Updated 29 October 2026

Short answer: Aim for 300 DPI at the final canvas size: Gelato, Prodigi, Printful and Printify each name 300 DPI as ideal (checked 2026-10-29), and Gelato says files under 150 DPI are not recommended. Pixels = inches × DPI: a 16×20 in canvas needs 4,800 × 6,000 px at 300 DPI, or 2,400 × 3,000 at 150. For an image-wrapped canvas, add the depth to every side first: a 16×20 in canvas 1.5 in deep needs 19×23 in of picture.

So treat 300 DPI as the goal and 150 DPI as the floor. Between the two, a canvas usually still looks good from normal viewing distance, especially at large sizes.

Two things stand out. A 24×36 in canvas at 300 DPI needs a 7,200 × 10,800 px file, about 78 megapixels. And a 40×60 in canvas needs 6,000 × 9,000 px even at 150 DPI. That is why large canvas is where most files run short.

Add the wrap before you multiply

With image wrap, the picture continues round the sides, so the file must cover the front plus the depth on every side. For a 1.5 in deep canvas, add 3 in to each dimension:

Providers also add bleed. Gelato's own example: a 200 × 300 mm front on its slim 2 cm canvas needs a 298 × 398 mm file, and on its 4 cm canvas 310 × 410 mm (Gelato Support, checked 2026-10-29). Use your provider's template if it has one.

Mirror wrap and plain white or black sides are different: the provider fills the sides itself, so you only need the front. Our guide to canvas wrap edges explains the options.

Why big canvases can use fewer DPI

Nobody reads a 40×60 in canvas from 30 cm away. People look at it from across a room, and the canvas weave softens fine detail anyway. That is why 150 to 200 DPI is common for large canvas, while a small print held in the hand needs closer to 300.

Gelato adds one more canvas tip: because canvas has its own natural texture, use files without a built-in texture, which can clash with the weave (checked 2026-10-29). If your file falls below 150 DPI at a size, don't offer that size. A smaller canvas that prints sharply is better than a big one that prints soft.

Upscaling: what it can and can't do

Upscaling software adds pixels, so the DPI number goes up. It can't add detail that was never in the file; at best it makes a convincing guess. Before you sell a size from an upscaled file:

  • View it at 100% and check faces, lettering, fine lines and texture for smearing, halos or invented detail.
  • Compare it with the original, side by side, at the same zoom.
  • Order one sample at the largest size you will sell.

Also note that changing the "DPI" or "PPI" number in a file's settings without changing its pixel size does nothing to print quality. Only the pixel width and height count.

Check your file in three steps

  1. Read the pixel size. For example, 4,000 × 5,000 px.
  2. Divide by each size in inches. At 16×20 in that file gives 250 DPI; at 24×30 in it gives about 167 DPI.
  3. Drop any size below your floor. With a 150 DPI floor, this file covers 4:5 canvases up to 24×30 in, and should not be sold larger.

Questions

Is 150 DPI OK for a canvas print?

For large canvases seen from across a room, usually yes; it is the floor Gelato sets. Providers recommend 300 DPI, so aim higher for small canvases viewed up close.

What resolution do I need for a 24×36 canvas?

7,200 × 10,800 px for 300 DPI, or 3,600 × 5,400 px for 150 DPI. Add the wrap if the image continues round the sides: 27×39 in for a 1.5 in deep canvas.

Do I need 300 DPI for a 40×60 canvas?

That would be 12,000 × 18,000 px, which few files reach. Many sellers print large canvas at 150 to 200 DPI, which is 6,000 × 9,000 to 8,000 × 12,000 px.

Does mirror wrap need a bigger file?

No. With mirror wrap or plain sides, the provider fills the sides, so the file only needs to cover the front. Image wrap needs the extra.

How do I change the DPI of my image?

Changing the DPI setting alone does nothing. Print quality depends on pixel width and height, so you need a bigger file or a smaller print.
