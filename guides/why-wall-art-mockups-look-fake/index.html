Why wall art mockups look fake, and the fixes you can measure

Published by WallMock · Updated 29 October 2026

Short answer: Wall art mockups look fake when the art doesn't obey the room: its edges don't follow the wall's lines, its size doesn't suit the furniture, the light on it doesn't match the wall, its shadow is missing or falls the wrong way, or its whites are a different colour from the room's light. You can measure each one with a ruler tool and a colour picker. Fix the room and the placement, never the artwork's own colours.

Buyers rarely say why a mockup looks wrong. They just scroll past it. This guide turns each "looks fake" problem into a check you can run on any image before you upload it, whether it came from a template or a canvas print mockup generator.

The five tells, and how to measure each

Open the mockup in any editor that shows pixel positions and colour values. Photoshop, GIMP, Photopea and the colour picker built into most operating systems all work. Then run these five checks. The pass rules are our working rules of thumb, not industry standards.

Perspective: the art has to sit on the wall

The fastest tell is art that floats in front of the wall like a sticker. Draw a straight line along the top of the frame and another along the nearest horizontal edge in the room, such as a shelf, a dado rail or the top of a sideboard. In a room photographed straight on, both lines should be level. The frame's sides should be as upright as the nearest door frame.

In a room shot at an angle, extend the frame's top and bottom edges and the wall's lines until they meet. They should meet at the same point. If they don't, the art was pasted flat onto an angled wall. You should also see a sliver of the frame's side, and only on the side facing the camera.

Fix: use straight-on rooms for the first photo, where buyers judge fastest. For angled rooms, place the art using four corner points on the wall plane rather than by eye. Our guide to wall art placement covers choosing the usable wall.

Shape and scale: the furniture vouches for the size

First check the shape. Measure the art's width and height in pixels and divide. A 2:3 print should come out at 0.667, give or take 1%. If it reads 0.75, the art was stretched or cropped into a 3:4 template, and the buyer will notice when the print arrives.

Then check the size against the furniture. Measure the sofa, bed or sideboard below in pixels and divide the art's width by it. A worked example: a sofa 620 px wide with art 300 px wide gives 0.48. The art looks small and timid. Our guide to how high and how wide to hang art over a sofa suggests two thirds to three quarters of the furniture's width for a single piece or a group. Here that is about 410 to 465 px. The free art size calculator does the same sum in inches or centimetres.

Room scale in a mockup is an estimate from the photo, not a measured wall, so don't promise exact print sizes from a room image alone. Use the furniture to make the size believable, then state the actual sizes in a separate sizes image. Light and shadow: one light source, one story

Find the window or lamp. Use the colour picker on the wall a little to the left of the art and a little to the right. If the left reads 205 and the right reads 165, light falls off from left to right. Now sample similar tones at the art's left and right edges. If they read the same, the art looks lit by a different sun.

The honest fix is to choose a room where the light across the wall is soft and even, rather than repainting your art. A gentle, uniform shading over the whole artwork is how a photograph behaves. A tint or glow that changes the art's colours is not.

Then check the shadow. Shadows of the sofa and the lamp point away from the light, and the frame's shadow must too. Depth decides its width. A floating-frame canvas stands well off the wall and casts a soft, wider shadow. Inside its frame you also see a thin dark gap around the canvas. A slim framed print casts a narrow, crisp line. No shadow at all is the clearest sign of a paste-on. Our frame and mat proportions guide explains face, depth and the floating gap.

Colour and sharpness: the tells at 100% zoom

Sample a neutral patch of wall near the art and note red minus blue. A cream wall in warm evening light might read 225, 212, 195: red is 30 higher than blue. A white area in your artwork might read 246, 246, 246, a difference of zero. Put side by side, the art looks cut out of another photo.

Don't solve this by warming your art. Etsy's image rules say photos must accurately reflect the item's true appearance, including colour (Etsy, checked 2026-10-29). Choose rooms lit closer to daylight for your hero image, and keep warm rooms for later photos.

Last, zoom to 100%. The art should be about as sharp as a cushion or vase at the same distance. Art much crisper than everything around it looks pasted. Art much softer looks like a low-resolution file. Check your file against the largest size you sell with the print resolution checker, and export mockups large enough not to be blurry when buyers zoom.

A pre-upload checklist

  • The art's edges are parallel to the room's lines, or meet at the wall's vanishing point.
  • Art width ÷ height matches your file to within 1%.
  • The art is about two thirds to three quarters as wide as the furniture below.
  • Light falls across the art the same way it falls across the wall.
  • There is a shadow, on the side away from the light, sized for the product's depth.
  • A floating-frame canvas shows its gap; a framed print shows its mat if you sell one.
  • The wall's light is close to neutral, and the art's colours are untouched.
  • At 100% zoom, the art is as sharp as nearby objects.
  • At thumbnail size, you can still recognise the artwork.

Questions

Why do my wall art mockups look fake?

Usually one of five things: perspective that doesn't follow the wall, a size that doesn't suit the furniture, light that doesn't match the wall, a missing or wrong-way shadow, or art whites that are a different colour from the room's light.

How big should the art look in a mockup?

For a single piece or a group above furniture, about two thirds to three quarters of the furniture's width. Measure both in pixels and divide.

Should I change my art's colours to match the room?

No. Etsy requires photos to show the item's true colour. Choose a room with neutral, even light instead of tinting the artwork.

Does a framed canvas mockup need a shadow?

Yes. A floating-frame canvas stands off the wall, so it casts a soft shadow, and its gap shows as a thin dark line inside the frame. No shadow makes it look pasted on.

How do I check a mockup's perspective?

Draw lines along the frame's edges and along nearby room edges. In a straight-on room they should be parallel. In an angled room they should meet at the same vanishing point.
