Why wall art mockups look fake, and the fixes you can measure

By AK, founder of WallMock and an Etsy wall-art seller since 2023 · Updated 14 November 2026 · How we write our guides

Short answer: Most fake-looking mockups fail on the room before the art: a wide, empty, far-away room nobody lives in, or art hung where no one would hang it, across a moulding or centred on a stool. After that come five tells you can measure with a ruler tool and a colour picker: perspective, scale, light, shadow and colour. Fix the room and the placement, never the artwork's own colours.

A mockup can look wrong even when the artwork file is perfect. Below is what I look for in my own listing photos, what we learned reviewing all 518 WallMock rooms, and a check for each tell that works on any image, from a template or a canvas print mockup generator.

The room is the first tell

The wide, sterile room with the art small in the middle of a huge wall is the classic mockup look, and I rejected those rooms for my shop. Etsy buyers rarely see rooms like that. They look like a render because nobody lives in them.

What works is closer and warmer:

  • Close framing. The art is large in the photo, roughly 40–70% of the frame, with only the top of the sofa, bed or console showing.
  • A lived-in room. Glowing lamps, layered pillows and throws, plants, sheer curtains.
  • Walls people actually have. Warm beige, cream or light-grey plaster, panel moulding, shiplap. Charcoal with lit shelves for darker, moodier pieces.

Most framed-canvas buyers hang art in cream or warm-white rooms, and that is where it looks best. Across a shop I aim for about 60% cream or greige neutral rooms, 25% soft tints and 15% bold rooms, kept for subject art: seasonal, kids, cultural. A gallery of bold rooms reads as styling, not as somebody's home.

The spot is the second tell

Art in the wrong place looks fake even when perspective and light are perfect. When we reviewed all 518 WallMock rooms in November 2026, most of the default spots we moved were in rooms where the wall's own design (panels, wainscoting, niches) already marked where art belongs. Put the art inside that area, centred. Never across a moulding, never resting on a rail. With no wall design to follow, centre it on the sofa, bed or sideboard group, not on a stool or a single chair. On a plain wall, leave about 15–25 cm (6–10 in) above a sofa back and 20–30 cm (8–12 in) above a headboard. The placement guide has the full set of rules.

The five tells, and how to measure each

Once the room and the spot are right, open the mockup in any editor that shows pixel positions and colour values (Photoshop, GIMP, Photopea or your operating system's colour picker) and run five checks. The pass rules are our working rules of thumb, not industry standards.

Perspective: the art has to sit on the wall

The fastest tell is art floating in front of the wall like a sticker. Draw a line along the top of the frame and another along the nearest horizontal edge in the room: a shelf, a dado rail, the top of a sideboard. In a straight-on room both lines are level, and the frame's sides are as upright as the nearest door frame.

In an angled room, extend the frame's top and bottom edges and the wall's lines until they meet. They should meet at the same point. If they don't, the art was pasted flat onto an angled wall. You should also see a sliver of the frame's side, and only on the side facing the camera.

Fix: use a straight-on room for the hero photo, where buyers judge fastest. For angled rooms, place the art by four corner points on the wall plane, not by eye. Our guide to wall art placement covers choosing the usable wall.

Shape and scale: the furniture vouches for the size

Check the shape first, in a front-on view. Measure the artwork bounds, excluding the frame, and divide width by height; in an angled view, rectify the artwork plane before comparing. A 2:3 print should come out at 0.667, give or take 1%. If it reads 0.75, the art was stretched or cropped into a 3:4 template, and the buyer will notice when the print arrives.

Then check the size against the furniture. A worked example: a sofa 620 px wide with art 300 px wide gives 0.48, so the art covers about half the sofa. That can be right for a smaller piece you actually sell. Our guide to how high and how wide to hang art over a sofa suggests two thirds to three quarters of the furniture group's width; here that would be about 410 to 465 px. Don't enlarge a small product just to hit it. The free art size calculator does the same sum in inches or centimetres.

Room scale in a mockup is an estimate from the image, not a measured wall, so state your actual sizes in a separate sizes image. In WallMock you can enter the artwork's real size and, in 332 rooms with an estimated wall scale, have it drawn to that scale.

Light and shadow: one light source, one story

Find the window or lamp. Use the colour picker on the wall a little to the left of the art and a little to the right. If the left reads 205 and the right reads 165, light falls off from left to right, and the art should not look evenly lit against that. Sampling the art's own edges is only a clue, because the picture's content varies in brightness too.

Don't repaint your art to match. Choose a room where the light across the wall is soft and even. A gentle, uniform shading over the whole artwork is how a photograph behaves; a tint or glow that changes the art's colours is not.

Then the shadow. The sofa's and lamp's shadows point away from the light, and the frame's must too. Depth sets its width. A floating-frame canvas stands well off the wall and casts a soft, wider shadow, with a thin dark gap visible around the canvas inside the frame. A slim framed print casts a narrow, crisp line. No shadow at all is the clearest sign of a paste-on. The frame and mat proportions guide explains face, depth and the floating gap.

Colour and sharpness: the tells at 100% zoom

Sample a neutral patch of wall near the art and note red minus blue. A cream wall in warm evening light might read 225, 212, 195: red 30 above blue. A white area in your artwork might read 246, 246, 246, a difference of zero. The clash can look odd, but a cream wall and a white part of a picture are different references, so treat it as a clue rather than proof.

Never solve it by warming your art. Etsy's image rules say photos must accurately reflect the item's true appearance, including colour (Etsy, checked 2026-10-29). Use a room lit closer to daylight for the hero and keep warm rooms for later photos.

Last, zoom to 100%. The art should be about as sharp as a cushion or vase at the same distance. Much crisper than everything around it looks pasted; much softer looks like a low-resolution file. Check your file against the largest size you sell with the print resolution checker. If it falls short, upscale the print master. I never upscale the mockup itself. I check every listing image at 100% (corners, edges, signatures, small marks) and nothing with a known defect goes up.

A pre-upload checklist

  • The room is close and lived-in, with the art large in the frame.
  • The art sits inside the wall's panel, field or niche, or is centred on the real centrepiece.
  • The art's edges are parallel to the room's lines, or meet at the wall's vanishing point.
  • Source proportions are preserved in front-on artwork bounds, or checked after rectifying an angled art plane.
  • The placement suits the offered product size; any furniture-width styling guideline is optional.
  • Light falls across the art the same way it falls across the wall.
  • There is a shadow, on the side away from the light, sized for the product's depth.
  • A floating-frame canvas shows its gap; a framed print shows its mat if you sell one.
  • The wall's light is close to neutral, and the art's colours are untouched.
  • At 100% zoom, the art is as sharp as nearby objects.
  • At thumbnail size, you can still recognise the artwork.

Check one export without changing the source

  1. Open the final export beside the source file. Check the whole composition, then a recognisable detail such as lettering or a thin line.
  2. For a front-on example, compare artwork bounds, not the outer frame. In an angled view, don't apply the raw width-to-height test.
  3. Look at the whole scene at listing size for clipping, distracting objects and a misleading sense of scale, then at the downloaded file at 100% for edge defects.

Questions

Why do my wall art mockups look fake?

Often the room or the spot comes first: a wide, empty, far-away room, or art hung across a moulding or centred on a stool. After that, one of five things: perspective that doesn't follow the wall, a size that doesn't suit the furniture, light that doesn't match the wall, a missing or wrong-way shadow, or art whites that are a different colour from the room's light.

How big should the art look in a mockup?

Two thirds to three quarters of the furniture width is a styling starting point, not a size proof or a realism requirement. Show the product size you actually sell and label it separately.

Should I change my art's colours to match the room?

No. Etsy requires photos to show the item's true colour. Choose a room with neutral, even light instead of tinting the artwork.

Does a framed canvas mockup need a shadow?

Yes. A floating-frame canvas stands off the wall, so it casts a soft shadow, and its gap shows as a thin dark line inside the frame. No shadow makes it look pasted on.

How do I check a mockup's perspective?

Draw lines along the frame's edges and along nearby room edges. In a straight-on room they should be parallel. In an angled room they should meet at the same vanishing point.
