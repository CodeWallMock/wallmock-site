What wall art mockups really cost: Etsy packs vs subscriptions vs per-room pricing

Published by WallMock · Updated 29 October 2026

Short answer: Compare cost per finished listing image, not the price tag. A one-off pack costs its price divided by the images you'll actually make from it, plus your editing time. A subscription costs its monthly price divided by the images you make that month. Per-room or per-image pricing is cheap for a few images and grows with every one. For most shops, time is the biggest cost. WallMock costs $5 a month for 200 images, or $12 for unlimited clean 4K (4096 px) images.

Mockup prices are easy to compare and hard to judge, because the real cost hides in time and reuse. This guide gives you one formula for every pricing model and works through an example with WallMock's plans and prices.

What packs cost on Etsy: we looked at the first page of an Etsy search for "framed canvas mockup bundle" on 2026-10-29. Of 60 listings, shown in US dollars and many on sale, half were priced between about 5 and 15 dollars, the median was just under 9, and the largest bundles went over 100. Pack sizes ranged from a handful of scenes to over 2,000. That's one search on one day, not a market survey.

One formula for every model

Cost per image = money per image + time per image.

  • Money per image = what you paid ÷ the images you actually made with it. For a pack, divide by every image across every collection you'll use it for. For a subscription, divide the month's price by that month's images, not by the plan's cap.
  • Time per image = minutes per image ÷ 60 × what an hour of your time is worth.

Count all the time: placing the art, checking the crop and colour, exporting, renaming, and redoing the ones that went wrong. Our guide to PSD frame mockups vs an online generator shows how to time it.

Hidden costs that change the answer

  • Ratios. A pack made for 4:5 doesn't fit 2:3 or 3:1 art. Several ratios mean several packs. See the print ratio guide.
  • Wrong product. A slim poster frame in a framed canvas listing invites returns.
  • Licence limits. Client work or ads may need a different licence; see our mockup licence guide.
  • Refreshes. Seasonal hero swaps and new rooms repeat the work each time.
  • Cancelling. Check whether images stay licensed after you stop paying.
  • Same scenes as competitors. Popular packs and provider scenes appear in many shops.

How to lower your cost per image

  • Reuse a small set of rooms. Five or six rooms that suit your shop, used for every collection, spread the setup cost and make the shop look consistent.
  • Make fewer, better images. Eight images that each answer a buyer's question beat twenty near-duplicates. Our Etsy listing photo plan shows what each slot is for.
  • Batch by collection. Setting up once for 30 designs is far cheaper per image than setting up 30 times.
  • Preview before you pay. Use free previews to reject rooms that don't suit the art, so you only make images you'll use.
  • Size the plan to your real month. Count the images you actually made last month, and choose the plan that covers that, not the biggest one.
  • Go yearly once your volume is steady. Yearly prices are lower per month, but only worth it if you'll keep listing.

Questions

How much do wall art mockups cost?

It depends on the model and your time. On one Etsy search we ran on 29 October 2026, half the framed canvas mockup bundles cost about 5 to 15 US dollars. Subscriptions charge monthly. Compare cost per image, including your time.

Are free mockups really free?

They cost nothing up front, but you still pay in time, and some need attribution or limit commercial use. Other shops may use the same scenes.

Is a mockup subscription worth it?

If you list new designs regularly, usually yes, because the setup is reused and the time per image drops. For a few designs a year, a pack can be cheaper.
