Mockup licences and commercial use: can you sell with bought or free mockups?

Published by WallMock · Updated 29 October 2026

Short answer: Usually yes, for selling your own artwork, but the licence decides. Most mockup licences let you show your design in the scene on listings, stores and ads, and forbid reselling the empty scene or turning it into your own templates. Free mockups vary the most, so check for attribution and limits. Etsy adds its own rule: a stock mockup for a print-on-demand item must be authorised by its owner. This is general information, not legal advice.

Every mockup has two owners: you own the artwork, and someone else owns the room scene, the frame render or the photograph. Here is what to check in a mockup licence, and how WallMock's own terms work, before you use images from any Etsy mockup generator or template pack.

Two sets of rights in one image

A mockup combines your artwork with someone else's work. You need permission for both.

  • The artwork. You must own it or have the right to sell it. If you used AI, a photo or a font, check those terms too. Our guide to selling AI art prints on Etsy covers the art side.
  • The scene. The room photo, the 3D render or the frame belongs to whoever made it. Your licence from them says where and how you may show it.

Most problems come from the second part: a scene used in a way its licence doesn't cover, such as client work, a resold template or a missing credit.

Licences differ, so treat this table as a list of questions to answer from your own licence, not as answers.

If the licence is missing or vague, ask the seller in writing and keep the answer with the files.

Bought, free and generated mockups: where the risk sits

  • Template packs bought on Etsy or elsewhere. The licence is usually in the listing or a text file in the download. Save both. Some packs come in a personal-use and a commercial-use version, so check which one you bought.
  • Free mockup sites. Free to download is not the same as free to use in a shop. Many ask for credit; some forbid use in ads or on marketplaces.
  • AI-generated rooms. Check the image tool's terms for your plan. Some tie commercial use to a paid tier. Never let the tool redraw your art: see are AI mockups allowed on Etsy.
  • Print provider mockups. Printify, Printful and Gelato give you mockups of their products. Read each provider's terms before reusing them outside the product they show.
  • Mockup tools and apps. The licence usually covers images you download while your account is active. Check what happens after you cancel or if the service closes.

Platform rules on top of the licence

A licence lets you use the scene; the marketplace still decides what a listing photo may show.

  • Etsy. Listing photos must accurately represent what the buyer receives, and most items need at least one unedited photo. For print-on-demand items, Etsy allows a stock photo mockup if the stock photo's owner has authorised its use (Etsy Help, checked 2026-10-29). Your licence is the evidence of that authorisation, so keep it.
  • Printify. Custom mockups should closely match the real product. Our Printify mockups guide covers uploading your own.
  • Shopify. Your store is your responsibility. Keep the licence for every scene you use, in case a rights holder asks.

After you cancel, or if a service closes

Listing photos stay live for years, often far longer than any subscription you used to make them. So the most important clause for a seller is the one about time:

  • Does the licence outlive the subscription? Some tools say images you downloaded stay licensed after you cancel. Others tie use to an active plan. If the terms are silent, ask.
  • What if the service shuts down? A closed company can't answer questions later. Save a copy of the licence page, dated, with your downloads.
  • Do watermarked previews count? Almost never. Previews are for deciding, not for listing.
  • Who else can use the same scene? A licence is usually non-exclusive, so other shops may use the same room. Your art and your choice of rooms are what make your listings yours.

A licence checklist before you list

  • You own the artwork, or have the right to sell it.
  • You have the licence text for every scene, saved with the files.
  • It allows selling, not just personal use.
  • It covers every place you'll post: Etsy, Shopify, print-on-demand pages, ads.
  • It covers client work, if you make mockups for others.
  • You know whether a credit line is required, and where.
  • You know what happens to your rights if you cancel or the service closes.
  • The mockup matches the product: frame, mat, size and the artwork itself.

Questions

Can I use mockups I bought on Etsy for my own listings?

Usually, if the licence allows commercial use. Read the licence in the listing or download, save it, and watch for packs sold for personal use only.

Can I use free mockups commercially?

Sometimes. Free mockups have the widest range of terms: some need a credit, some forbid marketplaces or ads. Check the exact licence for each file.

Can I sell templates made from someone else's mockups?

Almost never. Most licences forbid reselling or giving away the scene, the PSD or a template made from it, even if you change it.

Can I use WallMock mockups on Etsy, Shopify and Printify?

Yes. WallMock's terms allow clean downloads in shop listings, online stores and print-on-demand product pages, as well as websites, social posts, ads and client presentations.

Can I keep using WallMock images after I cancel?

Yes. The terms say the licence to the room scenes and frames in your finished images lasts as long as you use them, including after you cancel or close your account.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is general information to help you read a licence. For a specific question about your rights, ask the licensor or a lawyer.
