Smartmockups is gone: what wall art sellers use now

Published by WallMock · Updated 29 October 2026

Short answer: Smartmockups.com closed on 27 September 2024, according to Canva's help centre, and the old address now redirects to Canva's mockups page. Mockups you exported before stay royalty-free for personal or commercial projects. For wall art, the alternatives are Canva's own mockups, Photoshop PSD templates, your print provider's mockup generator, or a wall art mockup generator built for framed canvas, framed prints and whole collections.

If your listing photos came from Smartmockups, here is what changed and how to choose a replacement for wall art, including WallMock's bulk mockup generator for whole collections.

What happened to Smartmockups

Smartmockups was a browser mockup tool that became part of the Canva group. Canva's help centre, read on 2026-10-29, says:

  • Smartmockups.com closed on 27 September 2024, after the closure was announced to its users in advance.
  • Smartmockups is part of the Canva group, which is now building mockup tools inside Canva.
  • All mockups previously exported from Smartmockups are royalty-free and can still be used in personal or commercial projects.
  • You may not redistribute or sell the empty mockups without your design, or use them as stock photos.

We also checked on 2026-10-29 that the old smartmockups.com address now redirects to Canva's mockups page. So your old exports are safe to keep using. Save copies of them now, with the date you made each one, because there is no longer an account to download them from.

What a replacement must do for wall art

A general mockup tool covers mugs, phones and T-shirts. Wall art has its own tests:

  • Product truth. A floating-frame canvas needs its gap and frame colour; a stretched canvas no frame and a wall shadow that shows its depth; a framed print its mat or no mat.
  • Your exact art. No redrawing, recolouring or "enhancing" of the file.
  • Your ratios. 2:3, 3:4, 4:5, 1:1, ISO A and long 3:1 panoramas, without cropping to fit a template.
  • Believable rooms with furniture that shows scale.
  • Volume. A collection of 30 designs in 8 rooms is 240 images.
  • Output size. Etsy recommends listing photos at least 2000 px in width and height (checked 2026-10-29).
  • A clear licence for listings, stores and ads. See our mockup licence guide.

A 20-minute test for any replacement

Before you move a whole shop, run the same test on every tool you're considering. Use one real artwork with small text or a signature, in your most common ratio.

  1. Make three mockups: a hero room, a close-up of the frame and a room with a sofa or bed for scale.
  2. Check the shape. Measure the art's width and height in pixels in each mockup and divide. It should match your file.
  3. Check the art. Zoom to 200% on the text and signature, and compare a flat area of colour with your file.
  4. Check the product. Is the frame the colour you sell? Does a floating frame show its gap, and a framed print its mat?
  5. Time a batch. Add five artworks and time how long it takes to get every one in the same three rooms.
  6. Read the licence for listings, ads and what happens if you cancel.

Write the results down in a small table. The tool that passes the checks and has the shortest batch time is usually the right one for a wall art shop. Our guide to why wall art mockups look fake explains each check in more depth.

Moving a Smartmockups workflow

  1. Keep what's live. Listings that already use Smartmockups exports can stay as they are; the licence for exported mockups continues.
  2. Collect your exports in one folder per collection, so you know which listings still use them.
  3. Choose new hero rooms that suit each collection, and one frame look per product.
  4. Redo one collection at a time, so each shop page stays consistent rather than mixing old and new styles.
  5. Replace the first photo first. It's the thumbnail, so it has the biggest effect.
  6. Keep a record of which tool and licence made each listing's photos, with the date, in the same folder as the files.

If you used Smartmockups for many product types, you may end up with two tools: a general one for mugs, apparel and devices, and a wall art tool for framed canvas, framed prints and posters. That's normal, because wall art needs product details a general tool rarely shows.

Questions

Is Smartmockups still available?

No. Canva's help centre says Smartmockups.com closed on 27 September 2024, and the old address now redirects to Canva's mockups page.

Can I still use mockups I made in Smartmockups?

Yes. Canva's help centre says previously exported Smartmockups mockups are royalty-free and can still be used in personal or commercial projects. You may not resell the empty mockups or use them as stock photos.

What replaced Smartmockups?

Canva says the Smartmockups team is now building mockup tools inside Canva. Other options are Photoshop PSD templates, print provider mockups and wall art mockup generators.

What is a good Smartmockups alternative for wall art?

One that shows the product you sell, such as a floating-frame canvas with its gap, keeps your art exact and your ratio, and can handle a whole collection. Compare tools on those points.

Does WallMock make mockups for T-shirts or mugs?

No. WallMock only makes wall art mockups: framed canvas, stretched canvas, metal prints, and framed prints or posters (wood frame or aluminum framed print) with or without a mat.
