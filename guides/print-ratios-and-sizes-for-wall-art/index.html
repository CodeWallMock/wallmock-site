Print ratios and sizes for wall art: 2:3, 3:4, 4:5, 11×14 and A sizes

Published by WallMock · Updated 28 October 2026

Short answer: Every print size belongs to a ratio family: 2:3 (4×6 to 24×36), 3:4 (18×24), 4:5 (8×10, 16×20), 11:14, ISO A (A4 to A1, about 1:1.41) and 1:1. A file prints at every size in its own family without cropping, and needs cropping or borders for any other. For 300 DPI, multiply the inches by 300: a 24×36 inch print needs 7200×10800 pixels.

Every print size belongs to a ratio. Artwork made at one ratio prints at every size of that ratio without cropping, but needs cropping or white borders for any other ratio. Knowing the families saves refunds and confused buyers, and lets you show poster mockups at the right shape for each size you sell.

What a ratio is

A ratio compares the long side with the short side. An 8×12 inch print and a 24×36 inch print are both 2:3: the long side is one and a half times the short side. Any artwork made at 2:3 prints at either size without cropping.

An 8×10 print is 4:5, a different ratio. A 2:3 artwork printed at 8×10 loses part of its long side, or gains white borders. This is the most common source of 'my print was cropped' complaints.

Sizes by country

Buyers in the United States and Canada mostly think in inches: 8×10, 11×14, 16×20, 18×24 and 24×36 are everyday frame sizes. Buyers in the UK, Europe, Australia and India more often frame in A sizes (A4, A3, A2) or in centimetre poster sizes such as 30×40, 50×70 and 70×100 cm.

If you sell internationally, list both inches and centimetres, and include an ISO A file with digital downloads.

What digital-download sellers usually include

Many printable wall art listings include five files so the buyer can print most standard sizes: 2:3, 3:4, 4:5, 11×14 and an ISO A file. Each file is prepared at its own ratio from the original artwork, by extending the background or adjusting the composition, never by stretching. Name the files by the sizes they print (for example 'ratio-2x3-prints-4x6-to-24x36.jpg') so buyers don't have to work it out.

Pixels needed at 300 DPI

Multiply the inches by 300 for the pixels needed at 300 DPI, the usual standard for prints seen up close. At large sizes many sellers accept 150–200 DPI because large prints are viewed from further away, but say clearly what you supply.

Questions

Is 8×10 the same ratio as 16×20?

Yes, both are 4:5. 11×14 is a different ratio (11:14).

Is A4 the same ratio as A3 and A2?

Yes. All ISO A sizes share the ratio 1:1.414, so one A file prints at every A size.

What resolution do I need to print 24×36 inches?

7200×10800 pixels at 300 DPI. Many sellers accept 150–200 DPI for prints this large because they are viewed from further away.

Which ratio should I make my artwork?

2:3 covers the most common inch sizes; add an ISO A version for buyers outside North America. If you sell digital downloads, prepare a file for each main ratio.
