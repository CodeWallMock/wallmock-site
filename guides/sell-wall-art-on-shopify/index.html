How to sell wall art on Shopify with print on demand

Published by WallMock · Updated 29 October 2026

Short answer: Install a print-on-demand app such as Printify, Printful or Gelato from the Shopify App Store, create each artwork as a product in the provider's app, and publish it to your store. Shopify allows up to three options and 2,048 variants per product, so size × frame colour fits in one product. Then replace generic provider images with room photos that match each frame and size, around 2048 × 2048 px.

Shopify gives you your own store; a print provider makes and ships each order. What actually sells the piece is the photos, so plan them alongside the product, with a bulk mockup generator for whole collections.

How the pieces fit together

A print-on-demand wall art store on Shopify has three parts:

  1. Your Shopify store: the theme, product pages, collections and checkout.
  2. A print-on-demand app: its catalogue of canvases, frames and posters, its prices, and fulfilment. When an order comes in, the app passes it to the provider, which prints and ships it.
  3. Your photos: room mockups, a close-up of the product and, ideally, a photo of a sample.

Printify, Printful and Gelato all have apps in the Shopify App Store (Printify, Printful, Gelato, checked 2026-10-29). Each listing names wall art among its products and a mockup generator among its features. Our guide to choosing a print-on-demand provider for wall art compares what each one makes and where it prints.

Choose the products: framed canvas, framed prints, posters

Decide which products you'll offer before you build a single page, because each needs different photos.

Providers make these differently, so read the product page. Your mockups should show the same finish and proportions. Our guide to canvas vs framed print mockups helps match product to artwork.

Set up products and variants

Shopify's help centre says a product can have up to three options and up to 2,048 variants (Shopify, checked 2026-10-29). For wall art, the usual options are size and frame colour.

A worked example: one artwork offered in 5 sizes and 4 frame colours is 5 × 4 = 20 variants, well inside the limit. Add a third option, such as "with mat" or "without mat", and it becomes 40.

Two ways to organise a catalogue:

  • One product per artwork and product type, such as "Misty Mountains, framed canvas". Clear pages and photos that match exactly. Best when canvas and prints sell at very different prices.
  • One product per artwork, with product type as an option. Fewer pages, but every photo must make clear which version it shows.

Shopify lets you add a different image to each variant (Shopify). Use it: when a buyer picks the walnut frame, the main photo should change to a walnut-frame mockup. If your provider's app creates the variants for you, check their names and images after publishing.

Image sizes for Shopify product photos

From Shopify's help centre (product media types, checked 2026-10-29):

  • Product images must be smaller than 20 MB.
  • They can be up to 5000 × 5000 px, or 25 megapixels.
  • For square product images, 2048 × 2048 px usually displays best.
  • PNG is the best file type for most product images, followed by JPEG.

Keep every main image in a collection the same shape, usually square, so collection grids line up. A square mockup at 2048 px or more gives buyers room to zoom. If you also sell on Etsy, the same square file works there; check it with the Etsy listing photo checker.

Plan the photos for each product page

Plan the photos by the questions a buyer has, in the order they ask them. A strong order for wall art:

  1. Hero room: the art large and clear in a calm, neutral room.
  2. Scale: the art above a sofa, bed or sideboard.
  3. Product detail: a close view of the frame corner, mat or canvas edge.
  4. The artwork flat: so buyers see every detail.
  5. Two to four other rooms: different styles and wall colours.
  6. Sizes: an image listing every size and frame.
  7. A sample photo: your actual product, unedited.

Our Etsy listing photo plan explains each slot; the same logic works on Shopify. Before you make any images, check the file's ratio with the print ratio guide so the art is never cropped to fit a provider size.

Make collections look like one brand

Shopify collection pages show the first image of each product in a grid. If every product's first image comes from a different template, the page looks like a marketplace. If they share two or three rooms and one frame look, it looks like a gallery. Pick a small set of hero rooms that suit the collection, and use them for every artwork in it. When you add a new piece later, it matches without extra thought.

Questions

Can you sell wall art on Shopify with print on demand?

Yes. Install a print-on-demand app from the Shopify App Store, create your artwork as products in it, and publish them to your store. The provider prints and ships each order.

Which print-on-demand apps work with Shopify for wall art?

Printify, Printful and Gelato all have apps in the Shopify App Store and list wall art among their products. Compare their frames, sizes and print locations before you choose.

How many variants can a Shopify product have?

Shopify's help centre says up to 2,048 variants and up to three options per product. Size, frame colour and mat are typical options for wall art.

What size should Shopify product images be?

Shopify says square images of 2048 × 2048 px usually display best. Images can be up to 5000 × 5000 px or 25 megapixels, and must be under 20 MB.

Should framed and unframed versions be one product or two?

Either works. Separate products keep photos and prices clear; one product with a product-type option means fewer pages, as long as each variant has its own matching photo.

Does WallMock upload images to Shopify?

No. WallMock makes the room images; you download them and add them to your Shopify products or variants.
