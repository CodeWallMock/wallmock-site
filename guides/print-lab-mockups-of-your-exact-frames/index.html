How print labs can give sellers mockups of their exact frames

Published by WallMock · Updated 6 November 2026

Short answer: Record each frame as it is made (face width, depth, profile, finish, mat options), render the seller's exact art into rooms with those frames, check every frame against your own product photos, then deliver the images where sellers list. WallMock is building this as a partner frame catalogue, in development; for a partner pilot, talk to us.

Your sellers' listings are where buyers first see your frames. This guide covers what a lab needs to record, check and deliver so those photos show the product you actually ship.

Why generic templates fall short for a print lab

A generic template shows a frame, not yours. The differences buyers notice first are the ones templates usually get wrong:

  • Width and colour. A thin black frame in the photo and a wide walnut one in the parcel.
  • Depth and shadow. Flat art pasted on a wall has no side, no gap and no shadow, so canvas and frame look the same.
  • Floating-frame gap. The gap between the canvas and a floating frame is what tells a buyer it is a floater.
  • Mat proportions and metal surfaces. Mat width, and gloss versus matte metal, change how the product looks in a room.

Step 1: write down your frame range

Before anyone renders anything, record each frame as it is made:

RecordWhy it matters
Face widthThe visible width of the moulding from the front, in mm.
DepthHow far the frame stands off the wall; it sets the side you see and the shadow.
ProfileFlat, rounded, stepped or box; floating frames also need the gap between canvas and frame.
Finish and colourWood species or paint colour, and the sheen: matte, satin or gloss. Metal frames: brushed or polished.
Mat optionsMat colours and widths, if framed prints come with a mat.
Canvas depthFor stretched or floating canvas, the bar depth you print on.
Metal surfaceFor metal prints: gloss, high gloss or matte, and the corner radius.
SizesThe sizes each frame comes in, so rooms can show the art at a believable scale.

Add a front photo and a corner close-up of each frame. They are what the mockups are checked against.

Step 2: keep the seller's art exact

Sellers are trusting you with their files. A mockup system should place the artwork as supplied, with no AI redrawing, repainting or upscaling, and should be able to show that it did. Ask for a check per image: WallMock compares the art in every room photo with the uploaded file. See how WallMock keeps your art exact.

Step 3: show the art at a believable size

A 30 × 40 cm frame and a 90 × 120 cm frame should not look the same above a sofa. Choose rooms whose scale suits the sizes you sell. In the WallMock web app, 332 library rooms have a published estimated wall scale, so the artwork can be drawn at its real size; the scale is estimated from the room photo, so check sizes against your product.

Step 4: check the frames against your product photos

  • Put each mockup next to your product photo of the same frame, at full size.
  • Look at a corner up close: the width, the side, the gap and the shadow.
  • Check the colour on a neutral screen. On-screen colours are close approximations of a finish, so judge them against the real frame, not against memory.
  • Sign off each frame before sellers see it.

Step 5: deliver images where sellers list

Sellers need the photos on their listings, not in a folder. The usual routes:

  • Your own system requests images through an API and receives them by webhook.
  • Store delivery adds images to the seller's products, hero first.
  • Downloads, for sellers who upload by hand.

With WallMock, the API is in early access, Send to Shopify is in beta and Send to Etsy is coming soon, pending Etsy's approval of our app. Downloads work in the web app today.

Questions

How can a print lab give sellers mockups of its exact frames?

Record each frame's face width, depth, profile, finish and mat options; render the seller's exact art into rooms with those frames; check every frame against your product photos; and deliver the images where sellers list, through an API or store integration.

Why not just use generic frame templates?

Generic templates show a frame, not yours. Width, colour, depth, the floating-frame gap and the shadow are usually different, and buyers notice when the parcel does not match the listing.

What should a lab record about each frame?

Face width, depth, profile, finish and sheen, mat colours and widths, canvas depth, metal surface and corner radius, and the sizes each frame comes in, plus a front photo and a corner close-up.

How do you know the seller's art was not changed?

Ask for a check on every image. WallMock compares the art in each room photo with the uploaded file, and never redraws the art.

Does WallMock offer this today?

Partly. WallMock renders its own frames and finishes today, and the API (early access) can carry your frame names and photo order. Presets of a partner's actual frames are in development, built in partner pilots; to discuss one, talk to us.
