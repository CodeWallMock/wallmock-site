Canvas vs framed print mockups: which to show for your art

Published by WallMock · Updated 28 October 2026

Short answer: Show the product you sell. A stretched canvas has no frame and its image wraps the edges, which suits painterly and large pieces. A floating-frame canvas leaves a visible gap between canvas and frame and reads as premium. A framed print with a mat feels classic and suits photography, botanicals and line art. For printables, show a simple frame with a mat, because that is how most buyers display them.

The same artwork can feel casual, gallery-like or classic depending on how it is presented. Show the product you sell, and choose the presentation that suits the art and the buyer. WallMock's canvas print mockup generator shows both kinds of canvas, and framed prints too.

Stretched canvas

A stretched (gallery-wrapped) canvas has no glass and no frame; the image runs around the edges of a wooden stretcher. It suits painterly, textured and contemporary work and large statement pieces, and it is light and easy to hang.

In a mockup, the visible edge and a soft shadow on the wall show the depth. If your canvas has mirrored or white edges instead of wrapped image, the detail image should show that.

Floating-frame canvas

A floating frame holds the canvas with a small gap between the canvas edge and the frame, so the canvas seems to float inside it. It reads as premium and modern and protects the canvas edges.

Natural oak and walnut feel warm and suit Scandinavian, Japandi and earthy rooms; black and white feel crisp and graphic; gold suits classic rooms and traditional paintings. Our guide to choosing a frame colour shows one artwork in eight finishes.

Framed print with a mat

A mat (passe-partout) adds a border of white or off-white card around the print and makes it feel considered and gallery-like. Photography, botanical studies, line art and smaller works benefit most. Work out the mat opening and the frame to order with the frame and mat size calculator.

A thin black or oak frame with a white mat is the safest choice for digital downloads and for prints sold unframed, because it matches the ready-made frames most buyers already have.

Framed products usually sell for more, and the mockup should help justify that by showing the frame clearly in at least one close view.

How to choose

Sell canvas? Show canvas. Sell prints? Show frames that match what you ship or recommend. Selling printables? Show a simple frame and a mat, because most buyers will frame them that way.

If you offer several products, keep the room the same and change only the product, so buyers compare like with like. In WallMock, each room lists the products it supports (stretched canvas, framed canvas and framed prints with a mat), and the same setup can be reused for a whole collection.

Questions

Is a floating frame the same as a regular frame?

No. A floating frame leaves a visible gap around the canvas so it appears to float; a regular picture frame overlaps the edge of the artwork, usually behind glass.

Which looks more premium in a listing?

Floating-frame canvas and matted framed prints usually read as more premium than an unframed canvas, but the right choice is the product you actually sell.

Should a printable be shown as a canvas?

Usually not. Most buyers print and frame a download behind glass, so a simple frame with a mat sets the right expectation.
