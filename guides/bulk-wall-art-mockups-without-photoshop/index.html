Bulk mockup workflow checklist: naming, previews, exports

Published by WallMock · Updated 28 October 2026

Short answer: Decide whether you are making separate images of many artworks or one set on a wall. Save a small shortlist of favourite rooms and one frame look, preview a pale, a dark and a colourful piece, and check the planned image count and any unavailable combinations. Then download and name the files by artwork and listing. No Photoshop templates are needed.

Mockups for twenty designs shouldn't take twenty afternoons. This checklist builds a repeatable collection workflow with favourite rooms, saved looks, preview checks and organised exports; it is how WallMock's bulk mockup generator is meant to be used.

Decide what the batch represents

A new collection may need several independent artwork images in the same room style. A coordinated pair or trio needs several artworks together in one image. Decide which output you are making before uploading. These workflows are different: multiplying artworks by rooms produces separate images, while a set keeps its numbered pieces together.

Build a small reusable room collection

Start with a few rooms that fit the product (a mix of neutral, tinted and bold rooms works for most shops): a clear product-led view, a contextual interior and a complementary setting for another shape or colour palette. A larger room library does not mean every room belongs in every batch. Save favourites so the next collection begins with a useful shortlist instead of another long search.

Save the choices that should stay consistent

Choose the available canvas or framed print product, frame finish and mat options for each room. Save a Profile or favourite setup when you want to reuse the look. Consistency helps a shop feel coherent, but do not force the same dark frame onto every artwork if it misrepresents what you sell. Room availability can change with the selected product and artwork ratio.

Preview before making final images

Check one pale, one dark and one colourful piece when the collection contains them. Look for weak separation from the wall, overly dominant frames, excessive empty space and distracting room details. Inspect the artwork boundaries as well as the whole scene. Previewing is a decision step: it is where you reject an unsuitable combination before producing a full batch.

Review the planned output count

In WallMock, several artworks can create separate singles in compatible chosen rooms, or you can place one pair, trio or set of four together. Read the planned image count and any unavailable combinations before generating. Unsupported room/product/proportion combinations should not be treated as successful outputs. A small clean batch is more useful than a large folder that needs extensive manual sorting.

Download, organise and check the result

WallMock provides 4096 px exports and downloads as individual JPEGs, per-artwork ZIPs or a batch ZIP. Keep the artwork name and intended listing together when organising your files.
