How WallMock keeps your art exact

Published by WallMock · Updated 29 October 2026

Short answer: WallMock is a mockup generator that doesn't change your art. WallMock places your file as it is: it is never redrawn, repainted or upscaled by AI. On the 61 room images in our published example set, the art matched its source file with a structural similarity of at least 0.99 out of 1 and an average colour difference of 0.72 or less, too small for most people to see. The room is a digitally staged scene; your art is exact.

This page shows what happens to your file, why it matters when you sell prints, how we measured it, and what to check in any tool. Every image below opens at full size, so you can look for yourself. More are on the wall art mockup examples page, and you can try the wall art mockup generator free.

What WallMock does with your file

WallMock places your file as it is: it is never redrawn, repainted or upscaled by AI. The frame, the mat, the shadow and the room are built around your artwork. The artwork itself comes from your file.

  • Same picture. Lettering, signatures, faces, fine lines and brush marks stay as they are in your file.
  • Same shape. Your art keeps its own proportions, from square to 3:1 panoramas and 1:3 tall pieces. It is never stretched or squashed to fit a room.
  • Same file for a set. In a set of 2, 3 or 4, each panel is its own file, placed as supplied.

The art is resized to fit the frame in the room, and the finished image is saved as a compressed image file, up to 4096 px. That changes how many pixels the art takes up, not what it shows.

Why exact art matters when you sell prints

Your buyer pays for the picture. If a mockup tool redraws it, the listing can show a slightly different picture from the print that arrives: a misspelled word in a quote print, a changed signature, a face that looks like someone else, a shifted brand colour. Each one is a reason for a return, a bad review or a message you have to answer.

Etsy's photo rules point the same way. Etsy's listing image rules (updated 22 October 2026): most listings need at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background, with no editing or AI tools on that photo. Other photos may be edited if they show the item accurately. Print-on-demand items made by a production partner may use an authorised mockup instead, and digital downloads need a preview of the content. So use WallMock images for your room and detail photos, and add one real photo of the print or canvas unless you sell print on demand or downloads. How we measured it on our examples

We checked the art in every image of WallMock's published example set: 61 room images made with WallMock from 30 artwork files, including sets, panoramas and tall pieces (the 6 close-ups on the examples page are crops of these images). Each panel of a set was checked on its own, so that is 68 placed artworks. Two checks ran on each one:

  1. The renderer's own check. The art area of the finished image is mapped back to a flat, straight-on view and compared with your file. To pass, it must score at least 0.90 for structural similarity and under 3 for average colour difference.
  2. A separate check. A second check, run apart from the renderer, compares the art area with the unmodified source file: structural similarity on brightness in small 8 × 8 pixel windows, and the average colour difference (CIE76 ΔE) across the art.

Structural similarity compares shapes, edges and fine detail: 1 out of 1 means identical (structural similarity). Colour difference measures how far apart two colours are: 0 means the same colour, and about 2.3 is usually given as the smallest difference most people can notice (colour difference, checked 2026-10-29).

The lowest renderer score, 0.9467, was one panel of a set; the separate check scored that same panel 0.9981. The two checks measure in slightly different ways, so their numbers differ a little; both are well clear of their pass lines.

Why a mirror control? A test that gives a high score to anything roughly similar proves nothing. So the separate check also compares the room image with a flipped and a rotated copy of each single piece. Across the 56 single pieces, the closest of those look-alikes scored 0.8184 at most, while the real file scored 0.99 or more. One artwork, an arch and sun, is almost the same when flipped, so at the standard 512 px size its mirror image scored too close for the rule to pass. It was measured again at 1024 px with the same pass lines, and passed. Both images of it are counted above, and its 1024 px results are the weakest separate-check scores in the table.

The separate check also measured the brightness of each colour channel, red, green and blue, against the file: every one was within 0.1%.

These numbers describe our published example set. They are not a check the app runs on each image you make, and we don't claim they are. What is the same for every image is how your file is placed: it is never redrawn, repainted or upscaled by AI.

The room is staged. Your art is exact.

Rooms are curated, digitally staged interiors reviewed by WallMock. The furniture, the light and the wall are a staged scene, chosen to show how your art could look in a home. They are not a photo of your print hanging somewhere.

That is why the two halves of a WallMock image deserve different trust. The room sets the mood. The artwork is the part your buyer is paying for, and that part is your file, placed as supplied. If you want buyers to see the physical piece too, Etsy's rule above already asks most shops for one unedited photo of the actual item.

What to check in any mockup tool

You don't have to take any tool's word for it, ours included. Before you list, make one mockup from a file with small text, a signature and one strong colour, then:

  • Download it at full size and open it next to your original. Zoom to 200% on lettering, signatures and faces.
  • Compare a flat area of colour. It should look the same, not warmer, cooler or brighter.
  • Look for added or missing detail: new brush strokes, smoothed lines, a changed texture.
  • Measure the art's width and height in the mockup and divide. The ratio should match your file (our guide to print ratios and sizes lists the common ones).
  • Make a set of two or three. Every panel should match its own file.
  • Check the product: the frame, the mat and the canvas should match what the buyer will get.
