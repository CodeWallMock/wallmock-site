Why I built WallMock

I sell the products that WallMock is made to show.

By AK · Founder of WallMock · 1 November 2026

I’m AK, the seller behind AntarmadaArtPrints on Etsy since 2023. I built WallMock because choosing rooms, matching frames and exporting the same designs again and again had become part of every collection. I wanted a workflow I could reuse, with the artwork kept intact and the product shown clearly. I now use WallMock for my own listings.

My shop came first

I run AntarmadaArtPrints, a print-on-demand wall art shop. I have sold on Etsy since 2023. By 1 November 2026, the shop’s public counter showed more than 8,000 art sales. You can visit the shop to see the products, the listing images and the current reviews.

A collection means more than uploading the artwork. Each design needs a room that suits it, the right frame, a useful crop and images for the options a buyer can order. Then the next collection needs the same work.

Showing the frame a buyer expects

I have had buyers question whether a frame matched the listing image. That feedback stayed with me. A narrow floating-frame gap, a different wood tone or a mat that is too wide can change the product someone thinks they are buying.

More recent buyers have told me that the piece matches the website, or looks better in person. Those messages keep me focused on the details: show the correct product, keep the artwork intact and make the options clear. You can read the shop’s current Etsy reviews for yourself.

Before listing a product you have not checked, order a sample. A digital frame colour is an approximation. Paper, canvas, ink and room light affect the physical result, so compare the supplier’s specifications and sample photos when preparing a listing.

The repeated decisions I wanted to save

I built WallMock around favourite rooms and saved frame looks. Once a room works for the shop, I want to reuse that setup for the next collection. The results still need review: a dark print, a panorama and a pale drawing will not suit every room equally.

The bulk workflow keeps that distinction clear. Save the repeated choices, preview the artwork, check the compatible output count and download the files before leaving the session. WallMock makes the mockups; I add them to the listings.

The picture and the product both matter

A room can be beautiful while showing the wrong product. I wanted separate choices for stretched canvas, floating-frame canvas, paper prints with a mat, and metal products. I also wanted lettering, signatures and composition to come from the uploaded file.

Our artwork comparison shows the recorded checks on a published example set. The testing page explains what those checks tell you, and what still needs a sample or a listing check.

I use WallMock in my own shop, and I built it for sellers doing the same repeated work. Try the file you actually intend to sell. Check the frame and artwork at full size, then look at the small thumbnail a buyer will see.

Questions

Who built WallMock?

Built by AK, the seller behind AntarmadaArtPrints on Etsy since 2023. He built the app around his own wall art listing workflow.

Can I see the founder’s Etsy shop?

Yes. The shop is AntarmadaArtPrints on Etsy. Its public page identifies AK as the owner and shows its trading history and current customer reviews.

What did selling prints teach you about mockups?

The frame, mat, canvas depth and artwork crop all change what a buyer expects. A good-looking room image still needs to represent the product in that listing.

Does a room mockup replace a physical sample?

A sample is the way to check print colour, material, frame finish and construction. Use a room mockup to show the design in context, and make the offered product and dimensions clear.
