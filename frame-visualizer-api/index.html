How would a shopper preview work?

Three steps on your own website. This is the plan for the API; it is not built yet.

Why would a preview help a print company?

A shopper choosing a frame for their own art usually sees it on a sample picture, or on a plain white background. They have to guess how their artwork will look in your frame, on a wall. A preview is meant to take the guesswork out of that choice.

  • Shoppers see the frame before they buy. Their own art, in the frame you will ship.
  • They compare frames on their own art. Black, wood or gold, with or without a mat, side by side.
  • Fewer surprises when the order arrives. The aim is that the frame on screen is the frame in the box.
  • More confidence to order. The aim is that a shopper who has seen the result feels ready to order.
  • They stay on your site. Comparing happens on your product page, not somewhere else.

These are the aims of the shopper preview. They have not been measured with a partner yet.

How does early access work?

  1. Tell us

    What you sell, your frame range, what your website runs on and roughly how many previews a month you expect.

  2. Share your range

    The frames you want to start with: profile, face width, depth, finish, mat options and product photos. A pilot starts with a handful of frames.

  3. Judge the samples

    We build presets of your frames and send sample images made with an artwork you choose. You compare them with your own product photos.

  4. Plan the preview

    If the frames look right to you, we agree how the preview fits your site. Terms are agreed with each partner.

How would shoppers' art be handled?

  • Exact art. Placed as supplied, never redrawn, with an exact-art check on every room photo. This is how WallMock works today.
  • Deleted after processing. Today, artworks are deleted after processing and never used for training. The shopper preview is planned to follow the same rule.
  • Your shoppers stay yours. WallMock makes images; it does not sell to your shoppers or take their orders.

Questions

Is there an API that lets our customers preview their art in our frames?

WallMock is building one. The frame visualizer API is in development and not available yet; early access is by request at hello@wallmock.com. It is planned to let your shoppers upload their artwork on your website and see it in your frames, in a curated, digitally staged room, before they order.

Is the shopper preview available now?

No. It is in development, with no launch date yet. What works today is the WallMock web app, where sellers make room mockups of their exact art with WallMock's own frames. The WallMock API for listing mockups is in early access, not available yet.

Can it show the frames we actually sell?

That is the plan. Presets of a partner's actual frame range are in development and are built in partner pilots from your frame details and product photos, then checked with you before shoppers see them. Today WallMock shows its own frames and finishes.

Will the shopper's artwork be changed?

No. WallMock places the artwork as supplied; it is never redrawn. Every room photo gets an exact-art check against the uploaded file. The shopper preview is planned to work the same way.

Will it work on our website?

The aim is a preview that runs on your own website through the API, whatever your site is built with. Tell us what your site runs on when you request early access, and we will say honestly what an early-access pilot can cover.

Can the preview carry our own brand?

Not yet. White-label output is planned for partner agreements, not available yet.

What happens to shoppers' uploads?

Today, WallMock deletes artworks after processing and never uses them for training. The shopper preview is planned to follow the same rule.

How fast will a preview be?

Not measured for the shopper preview yet. In development tests, the WallMock API takes about 3 to 5 seconds per 4K image per worker. Preview sizes and speeds are measured with each partner in a pilot.

What will it cost?

Prices are not published. Early-access terms are agreed with each partner.

How is this different from the WallMock API page?

The WallMock API page covers listing mockups that sellers and print companies make for their product listings. This page covers a preview for your shoppers: they see their own art in your frames before they order. Both are planned to use the same WallMock renderer.
