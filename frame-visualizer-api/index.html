Why would your shoppers want it?

Someone ordering a frame for their own art usually sees it on a sample picture or a plain white background, then guesses. The preview replaces that guess with their own artwork, in your frame, on a wall, at the size they are about to buy.

  • Confidence before they pay. Their own art, in the frame you will ship, in a room that looks like a home.
  • Compare frames on their own art. Switch between black, oak or walnut in a moment, instead of imagining it from a swatch.
  • Pick the right size. Switch from 12 × 18 to 24 × 36, drawn to scale, so a print that would look lost on the wall is caught before it is ordered.
  • Fewer surprises when the box arrives. The frame on screen is modelled on the frame you make.
  • They stay on your page. Comparing happens next to your Add to cart button, not in another tab.

These are the results we expect a preview to bring. We measure them with each pilot lab: add-to-cart rate on the upload page, which frames people choose, and frame-related returns.

What do you get?

Everything a shopper needs to choose a frame with confidence, set up for you.

01Your frames, built for you

We model your mouldings and finishes from your specs and product photos, the way we built WallMock's own frames. You approve them against your own photos before any shopper sees them. They stay private to you.

02Curated rooms

A set of curated, digitally staged rooms (4 to 12, by plan) chosen for your products, each checked at every size you sell.

03Drawn to scale

Every print size is drawn to the room's estimated wall scale, so a shopper sees what 16 × 24 in looks like over a daybed.

04Exact art

The shopper's file is placed as supplied, never redrawn or restyled. All 20 prototype previews passed our exact-art check, at computer and phone screen widths.

05Built for any website

One snippet of HTML, designed for Shopify, WordPress and WooCommerce, Wix or your own build. No app to install, no theme rebuild. Your platform is tested in the pilot.

06Follows your product options

The preview can follow your own frame and size pickers, and tells your page which frame the shopper chose, so Add to cart matches.

07Your branding

Your frame names, sizes and colours. A small "Preview by WallMock" line, removable on larger plans.

08Privacy by design

Drawn in the shopper's browser: their art is not uploaded to us for the preview. No shopper accounts, no tracking cookies.

09Locked to your site, capped bill

Previews show on your own domains, not on other websites, and billing has per-network limits. Set an optional monthly cap if you want a fixed bill.

Questions

What is the WallMock frame visualizer?

A preview for print labs, framers and wall-art shops. On your product page, a shopper uploads their own artwork, picks one of your frames and a print size, and sees it framed on a wall in curated, digitally staged rooms, drawn to scale, before they order. You add it to your website with one snippet.

Is it available now?

Not generally. It is in development: a working prototype runs on our own test pages, and early-access pilots are open by request at hello@wallmock.com. There is no self-serve sign-up and no general launch date yet.

How do we install it?

Paste one snippet, a <div> and a <script> line, onto your product page. On Shopify that is a Custom Liquid section in the product template; on WordPress an HTML block; on Wix an embed code element. Developers can also listen for the shopper's frame and size choices and keep the page's own options in step.

Which platforms does it work on?

It is designed for any website that lets you add HTML and a script: Shopify, WordPress and WooCommerce, Wix and custom-built sites. So far it has run on our own test pages in desktop Chrome. In a pilot we test it on your platform and staging site, including Safari and Firefox, before it goes on your live product pages. A no-code Shopify app block is planned later.

How are our frames set up?

You send profile drawings or sample photos, face width, depth, finish photos, mat options and sizes. We build your frames the way we built WallMock's own, then send a sheet with our renders next to your product photos. Nothing reaches shoppers until you approve it, and your frames stay private to your account.

What happens to shoppers' artwork?

The preview is drawn in the shopper's own browser, so their artwork is not uploaded to WallMock for it, and nothing is stored or used for training. There are no shopper accounts and no tracking cookies. The artwork is placed exactly as supplied, never redrawn.

How fast is it?

In our prototype test on a fast Mac laptop, four rooms appeared about 1.6 seconds after upload, switching frames took 0.03 to 0.09 seconds and a new print size about 1.4 to 2.0 seconds. Everything was served from that same computer, so internet download time is not included, and switches were timed after the first rooms had loaded. Real-world speeds, on phones and in Safari and Firefox, are measured in each pilot.

What does it cost?

A pilot is free for 60 days from when it goes live on your product pages, including up to 3 of your frames. After the pilot, plans are $49, $149 or $399 a month with 10,000, 40,000 or 100,000 previews (a preview is one shopper adding one artwork, with any switches for 30 minutes). Extra previews are $3 per 1,000, and previews never pause unless you set a cap. Paying yearly gives 2 months free. Extra frames are a one-time setup from $150 each. This is early-access pricing and may change.

How long does a pilot take to start?

We aim for about 3 to 4 weeks from your yes and your frame details to a test on your staging site. The free 60 days start when it goes live on your product pages.

Can it carry our own brand?

Yes: your frame names, sizes and colours. A small "Preview by WallMock" line sits under the previews and can be removed on larger plans.

How is this different from the WallMock API page?

The WallMock API page covers listing mockups that sellers and print companies make for their own product listings. This page covers a preview for your shoppers: they see their own art in your frames before they order. Both use the same WallMock renderer.
