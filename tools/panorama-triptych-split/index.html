Two ways to split

  • Split evenly. The image is cut into equal panels and nothing is lost. The gaps add to the width on the wall.
  • Keep the width, cut out the gaps. The overall width stays the same and the strips behind each gap are removed from the image, so horizons and lines carry straight across, as if seen through a window. Use it for landscapes, not for art with text or a subject near a gap.

Hanging the panels

Hang all panels at the same height with equal gaps. Centre the whole group, not the middle panel, over furniture; the gallery wall spacing calculator gives nail positions.

Questions

How do I split a panorama into a triptych?

Choose 3 panels and a gap. Split evenly to keep the whole image: a 60 × 20 in panorama becomes three 20 × 20 in panels and, with 2 in gaps, is 64 in wide on the wall. Or keep the wall width and cut out the gaps, so lines in the scene stay straight across them.

What gap should a triptych have?

2 to 3 inches (5 to 8 cm) between panels is common for living rooms; smaller gaps read as one piece, larger ones as three. Keep every gap the same.

Which split mode should I use?

Split evenly when no part of the art may be lost, such as text or a centred subject. Cut out the gaps for landscapes and horizons, where the scene should look continuous behind the gaps.

Is my image uploaded?

No. The image is cut into panels in your browser and downloaded as one ZIP; nothing is sent anywhere.
