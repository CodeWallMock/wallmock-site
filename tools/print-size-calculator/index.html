How the calculator works

Your file's ratio is its long side divided by its short side. Print sizes come in families that share a ratio, and every size in your file's family prints without cropping. The calculator matches the file to a family within 0.4%, close enough for a pixel or two of rounding but tight enough to tell 5:7, A sizes and 7:10 apart. It then works out the DPI the file gives at each size, in your file's own orientation: a landscape panorama gets 36 × 12, not 12 × 36.

When a file matches no family, the calculator names the closest one and how much printing at it would crop, so you can crop on purpose or add a border. To check the DPI at sizes outside your family, with the crop each one needs, use the print resolution checker.

Making the file fit

  • Design at the ratio you sell. A 2:3 master file covers every 2:3 size from 4 × 6 to 40 × 60 inches; an A-series file covers A5 to A0.
  • Sell two families from one design by making a second crop, rather than letting a print shop crop it for you.
  • Match the frame. The standard frame sizes chart shows which sizes buyers can frame without a custom order.

Questions

What print sizes can I make from a 2:3 file?

Every 2:3 size prints without cropping: 4 × 6, 8 × 12, 12 × 18, 16 × 24, 20 × 30, 24 × 36 and 40 × 60 inches, and 20 × 30, 40 × 60, 60 × 90 and 100 × 150 cm. How large you can go depends on the pixels: at 300 DPI, a 4800 × 7200 px file reaches 16 × 24 inches.

Which sizes fit a 4:5 file?

8 × 10, 16 × 20, 24 × 30 and 32 × 40 inches, and 24 × 30, 40 × 50 and 80 × 100 cm. 11 × 14 is close but not the same shape (11:14), so a 4:5 file loses about 2% to cropping there.

Is 70 × 100 cm the same ratio as A sizes?

No. 70 × 100 cm is 7:10 (1:1.43); A sizes are 1:1.414. They are about 1% apart, so a file made for one crops slightly at the other. The calculator keeps them as separate families.

What sizes fit a 3:1 panorama?

12 × 36, 14 × 42, 16 × 48, 20 × 60 and 24 × 72 inches, or 30 × 90 and 50 × 150 cm, hung wide. A 9000 × 3000 px panorama prints 30 × 10 inches at 300 DPI and 60 × 20 inches at 150 DPI.

How many pixels do I need for a 24 × 36 print?

7,200 × 10,800 pixels for 300 DPI. Large prints seen from across a room are often printed at 150 to 200 DPI, which is 3,600 × 5,400 to 4,800 × 7,200 pixels.
