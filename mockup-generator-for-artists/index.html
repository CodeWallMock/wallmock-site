What an artist needs from a mockup

A shop selling generic decor can live with a rough mockup. You can't: your buyer is paying for your hand, so the photo has to show it.

Your marks, untouched

Brushwork, pencil texture, the edge of a wash and your signature should look the same in the room as in your file. Zoom in to 100% and check.

The product they receive

A canvas photo for a canvas, a matted frame for a work on paper, in the frame colour you ship. Anything else invites a return.

A room that suits the work

A moody oil reads well against a dark wall; a pale botanical needs light. Pick rooms for the painting, not the other way round.

Keep the colours true, from painting to photo

WallMock places your file as it is: it is never redrawn, repainted or upscaled by AI. That means the file you upload decides the colours in the mockup, so fix colour in the file, not in the room photo. Four habits keep it honest:

  1. Digitise in even light. Scan the work, or photograph it square-on in soft daylight with no glare. A lamp at an angle puts glare on varnish and thick paint, and a warm bulb shifts every colour.
  2. Compare on screen, next to the painting. Hold the original beside your monitor. If the scan's blues are colder, correct them before you upload anything.
  3. Save in sRGB for the web. Listing sites and phones show sRGB; a wide-gamut file can look dull or over-bright once it is online.
  4. Check a printed proof. Order one print of each new piece and compare it with the photo in daylight. Our sample check guide lists what to look at.

A room still changes how colours read, as any wall does at home. A sage wall can make the greens in a painting look flatter; a bright white wall can make soft pastels look paler. Choose rooms whose wall colour helps your palette. Our guide to room styles and wall colours has pairings.

Selling prints of your originals

Prints let more people own a piece of a painting you can only sell once. The photos should make it clear which is the original and which is the print, and show the print the way it will hang.

  • Check the file is big enough. A 16×20 in painting scanned at 600 DPI gives a 9,600 × 12,000 px file, enough for a 300 DPI print of 32×40 in. Put your own numbers into the free print resolution checker.
  • Fit the ratio to standard sizes. A 16×20 painting is 4:5, which prints at 8×10 and 16×20 without cropping, but not at 12×18. The print size calculator shows which sizes fit your shape.
  • Show the original and the print side by side in your shop. Same painting, same kind of room, different product. Buyers see at once that the print is the affordable version of the same work.
  • Say "print" in the first photo's caption and the title. Nobody should think they are buying the original for the price of a print.

A whole series at once

Artists work in bodies of work: twelve small seascapes, a set of botanical studies, a year of sketchbook pieces. Making each photo by hand, one painting at a time, adds up fast. In WallMock you set it up once:

  1. Heart the rooms that suit the series, say six calm living rooms and bedrooms.
  2. Save the look: for example a walnut floating frame for the canvases, or a white mat in a black frame for works on paper.
  3. Add the whole series. Each piece keeps its own proportions, from square to 3:1 panoramas and tall 1:3 pieces, and comes out in every chosen room.

Questions

What is a mockup generator for artists?

A tool that shows your artwork on a wall in a styled room, framed or on canvas, so buyers can picture it at home. For a painter or illustrator the key test is that your work appears exactly as you made it, with every brushstroke, line and signature intact.

Will a mockup change my painting?

Not in WallMock. WallMock places your file as it is: it is never redrawn, repainted or upscaled by AI. Some tools build the whole scene with AI, which can blur or redraw fine detail, so zoom in on any mockup before you list it.

Can I use mockups for an original painting on Etsy?

Yes, alongside at least one real photo. Etsy's listing image rules (updated 22 October 2026) ask most listings for at least one unedited photo of the actual item. Use room mockups for the other photos, as long as they show the piece accurately.

Which product should I show for a watercolour or ink original?

A framed print look with a white mat is closest to how works on paper are usually framed. Choose a wood frame or an aluminum framed print in the colour you will ship, or the frame you would recommend to the buyer.

Can I show a whole series at once?

Yes. Save your favourite rooms and frame once, add the series, and every artwork is made in every chosen room. A pair, trio or set of four can also hang together on one wall.
